## Thread B — E-Democracy as Immediate Public Check, Specified as Bounded Decision Right

The e-democracy as immediate public check is specified as a bounded decision right with the four-element format. The right operates over the public-check capacity to register objection to executive and legislative decisions in near-real-time at the ecosystem-and-habitat scale band, with the operational substrate articulated at depth in Document XII.03 and the formal bounded-decision-right architecture specified here.

**Scope.** The right operates over the public-check capacity to register objection to executive and legislative decisions in near-real-time. The scope is bounded: the right does not specify the substantive content of any particular objection, nor the institutional architecture by which objections trigger reconsideration (Document XII.03 specifies that operational substrate), nor the cross-scale-coupling specifications by which objections at ecosystem-and-habitat scale reach the foundational architecture at biosphere-and-ecozone scale (the coupling specifications are drafted into the cross-scale-feedback specifications the rights carry). The right specifies only the foundational architectural commitment that the population at ecosystem-and-habitat scale has the standing to register objection and to require institutional response in near-real-time, rather than being reduced to the participation-as-vote register the contemporary American constitutional architecture has substantially operated within.

**Scale.** Ecosystem-and-habitat. The public-check capacity is scale-matched to the decision being checked: decisions operating at ecosystem-and-habitat scale (watershed-management decisions, regional public-health decisions, municipal-and-county-scale infrastructure decisions, the broader regional-political-economic configuration) are checked through the public-check architecture at the corresponding scale. The cross-scale coupling specifies that decisions operating at finer scales (habitat-and-population, organism-and-organ-system) are checked under the architectures Documents XII.11 and XII.12 specify, and decisions operating at the foundational scale (biosphere-and-ecozone) are checked under the architecture Document XII.09 carries.

**Feedback condition.** The e-democracy architecture provides ongoing feedback through digital-deliberation infrastructure (the Pol.is-architecture cluster Document XII.03 engages, with the comparative-empirical archive across Taiwanese vTaiwan, Decidim Barcelona, the Madrid Decide platform, the Helsinki participation platforms, and the broader digital-deliberation record). The feedback condition specifies that the digital-deliberation infrastructure operates as continuous public-check architecture rather than as periodic-consultation architecture, with the architecture's performance monitored through the diverse-epistemic-standing monitoring Document XII.01's Thread G specifies. The feedback condition further specifies that the e-democracy architecture cannot operate without explicit engagement with the digital-divide dynamics that condition who can participate, the manipulation-vulnerability dynamics that condition the architecture's epistemic integrity, and the cross-scale-coupling dynamics that condition how ecosystem-and-habitat-scale deliberation reaches the foundational architecture.

**Sunset-or-adaptation condition.** The e-democracy platform architecture is subject to scheduled review and ongoing adaptive evolution under specified review architecture. The temporal scale of the review is matched to the dynamics the platform operates within: the technological substrate evolves at fast cycles, requiring rapid-adaptation conditions for the platform's technical architecture; the deliberative-cultural substrate evolves at slower cycles, requiring multi-year review conditions for the platform's deliberative-cultural commitments. The sunset-or-adaptation condition specifies that the platform is not engineered-fixture infrastructure but cultivation-condition architecture under continuous adaptive review, with the review architecture operating under the eco-mimetic legislative-cycle framework Document XII.01's Thread E carries.

## Thread C — Technological-Critique Response as Bounded Decision Right

The technological-critique response is specified as a bounded decision right with the four-element format. The right operates over the polity's capacity to register and respond to technological developments under conditions of cognitive plurality and feedback adequacy, with the operational substrate coordinated with Document II.06 (the cognitive-and-informational disturbance regime) and the formal bounded-decision-right architecture specified here.

**Scope.** The right operates over the polity's capacity to register and respond to technological developments — artificial intelligence, biotechnology, geoengineering, surveillance technology, and (per Thread F) weapons systems — under conditions of cognitive plurality. The scope specifies that the polity has the standing to deliberate on, register objection to, and require institutional response to technological developments before those developments are deployed at scales that foreclose deliberation. The contemporary configuration has substantially failed to maintain this standing: the technology-deployment cycle has accelerated past the deliberation-cycle, with technological developments reaching deployment at scales that foreclose deliberative engagement before the deliberation can be conducted. The right specifies that the polity's standing to deliberate is foundational architectural commitment that conditions the technology-deployment cycle rather than being conditioned by it.

Technological developments operating at ecosystem-and-habitat scale — regional-deployment artificial-intelligence systems, biotechnological developments with regional-ecosystem reach, geoengineering proposals at regional and continental scale, surveillance-technology deployments at municipal and metropolitan scale — are subject to the public-check architecture this right specifies. The cross-scale coupling specifies that developments at finer scales are subject to the architectures Documents XII.11 and XII.12 carry, and that developments at biosphere-and-ecozone scale (continental-scale geoengineering, global-scale artificial-intelligence systems) are subject to the foundational architecture Document XII.09 establishes.

**Feedback condition.** The technological-critique response operates through deliberative architecture coordinated with Document II.06 (the cognitive-and-informational-disturbance diagnostic) and with Document XII.03 (the e-democracy structural public check), and with the technology-assessment institutional traditions. **Sunset-or-adaptation condition.** The technological-critique response operates under rapid-adaptation conditions for fast-moving technological domains. The temporal scale of the sunset-or-adaptation condition is matched to the dynamics of the technology being assessed: artificial-intelligence systems evolve at fast cycles (months to years), requiring corresponding rapid-adaptation review conditions; biotechnological developments evolve at intermediate cycles (years to decades), requiring intermediate-adaptation review; geoengineering developments operate at longer cycles (decades to centuries), requiring longer-cycle review. ## Thread D — Public-Trust Threshold for Private Organizations as Bounded Decision Right

The public-trust threshold for private organizations is specified as a bounded decision right with the four-element format. The right operates over private-organization operation at scales where the operation crosses into public-trust terrain, specified through the public-trust doctrine and the contemporary fiduciary-and-public-trust literature, with the formal bounded-decision-right architecture specified here.

**Scope.** The right operates over private-organization operation at scales where the operation crosses into public-trust terrain. The scope specifies the architectural commitment that private organizations operating at scales of ecosystem-and-habitat reach — corporate operations whose substrate, externalities, or political influence reaches the ecosystem-and-habitat scale band — operate under public-trust accountability rather than under purely private-fiduciary obligation to shareholders. The scope is bounded: the right does not specify the substantive content of any particular public-trust obligation, nor the substantive criteria for crossing the threshold (those are conditioned by the comparative-public-trust scholarship and the case-by-case institutional record), nor the operational architecture by which public-trust obligations are enforced (that operates through the broader Part-XII architecture). The right specifies the foundational architectural commitment that the public-trust threshold operates as bounded decision right rather than as discretionary regulatory category.

**Scale.** Ecosystem-and-habitat, where private-organization operation reaches scales requiring public-trust accountability. The scale-matching specifies that the public-trust threshold operates at the band where private-organization operation has aggregate reach beyond the dyadic-private-contracting register the dominant contemporary American legal architecture operates within. Private organizations operating at finer scales are conditioned by the architectures Documents XII.11 and XII.12 carry; private organizations operating at biosphere-and-ecozone scale (global-scale corporations whose operations reach the foundational scale band) are conditioned by the foundational architecture Document XII.09 establishes and by the broader Part-XII architecture.

**Feedback condition.** The public-trust threshold operates through disclosure, audit, and adjudication architecture. The disclosure architecture specifies that private organizations operating at public-trust-threshold scale carry continuous disclosure obligations on their ecosystem-and-habitat-scale operations, with the disclosure specifications drafted into the right at the level of the relevant industry and jurisdiction. The audit architecture specifies that disclosure-information is subject to standing audit institutions with diverse-epistemic-standing audit authority (community-based audit, technical-scientific audit, deliberative-public audit) operating at parity. The adjudication architecture specifies that breaches of the public-trust threshold are subject to adjudicative review through the constitutional-and-statutory review architecture Document XII.01's Thread G articulates, with multi-disposition outputs (not just uphold-or-strike but amendment-trigger, adaptation-trigger, succession-trigger, panarchy-cycle-trigger) drafted into the review.

The public-trust doctrine has analytical depth in the legal literature beyond the contemporary American doctrinal default. Joseph L. Sax's "The Public Trust Doctrine in Natural Resource Law: Effective Judicial Intervention" (*Michigan Law Review* 68, 1970, 471–566) carries the foundational analytical articulation of the public-trust doctrine as legal-architectural commitment with substantive content across natural-resource governance. Sax's analytical work identifies the doctrinal substrate the Roman-law *res communes* tradition carried, the English common-law inheritance the United States Supreme Court engaged in *Illinois Central Railroad v. Illinois* (146 U.S. 387, 1892) and subsequent cases, and the substantive analytical commitments the doctrine carries for contemporary natural-resource governance. Mary Christina Wood's *Nature's Trust: Environmental Law for a New Ecological Age* (Cambridge University Press, 2013) extends the analytical work to the atmospheric-trust register, articulating the atmospheric-trust doctrine as legal-architectural commitment with implications across the climate-and-atmospheric governance terrain. The contemporary atmospheric-trust litigation archive — *Juliana v. United States* and the broader children's-trust litigation record — carries the contemporary doctrinal record on atmospheric-trust enforcement, with the litigation operating under substantial obstacles from the contemporary federal-judicial architecture but carrying substantive analytical commitments the project's broader architecture engages. Evan Fox-Decent's *Sovereignty's Promise: The State as Fiduciary* (Oxford University Press, 2011) supplies the fiduciary-political-philosophy literature's analytical apparatus on the state's fiduciary obligations to those over whom it operates, with the analytical work positioning the fiduciary framing as substantive constitutional commitment rather than as supplementary regulatory principle.

**Sunset-or-adaptation condition.** Public-trust threshold specifications are subject to scheduled review under the eco-mimetic legislative-cycle framework. The review cycle is matched to the dynamics of the industry and jurisdiction the threshold operates over, with rapid-cycle review for industries operating under fast-moving technological substrate and longer-cycle review for industries operating under slower-moving substrate. The sunset-or-adaptation condition does not specify automatic temporal sunset (the public-trust threshold is foundational architectural commitment); it specifies the conditions under which scheduled review and adaptive evolution are conducted under the eco-mimetic policy-making method Document XII.01 articulates.

The five public-check decision rights specified across the foregoing threads — e-democracy as immediate public check (Thread B), technological-critique response (Thread C), public-trust threshold for private organizations (Thread D), public-check rights over bodily, reproductive, and gender autonomy at ecosystem-and-habitat scale (Thread E), and the weapons-systems threading of the war-powers question (Thread F) — operate together as ecosystem-and-habitat-scale cultivation conditions under which the broader Part-XII architecture is cultivated. The rights operate in coupling: the e-democracy architecture provides the deliberative substrate the other rights operate through; the technological-critique response provides the engagement-with-emerging-developments capacity the other rights presuppose; the public-trust threshold provides the architectural commitment against which private-organization operations are checked at this scale band; the bodily-and-reproductive-and-gender-autonomy public-check provides the scale-matched check on the constitutive-administrative operations at this scale band; the weapons-systems threading provides the scale-matched engagement with weapons-system development at this scale band.

The cross-scale coupling operates through coordination with Document XII.09 (foundational decision rights at biosphere-and-ecozone scales) — the public-check architecture operates under the foundational architectural commitments Document XII.09 establishes, with the scale-matched public-check work conditioned by but not duplicating the foundational specifications. The coupling operates downstream with Document XII.11 (provisioning decision rights at habitat-and-population scales) — the provisioning architecture operates under the public-check architecture this document specifies, with the operational substrate of provisioning conditioned by the public-check work. The coupling operates with Document XII.12 (cognitive-and-intergenerational decision rights at organism-and-organ-system scale with temporal coupling) — the organism-scale operations operate under the cross-scale architecture the four D-series documents together specify.

Document XII.01 (Eco-Mimetic Policy-Making) supplies the operational method by which any of the public-check decision rights is drafted, enacted, monitored, and adapted; the present document specifies the substantive content of the rights, with the operational method drawn from Document XII.01 without duplication. Document XII.03 (E-Democracy and the Structural Public Check) supplies the operational substrate for the e-democracy architecture; the present document specifies the formal bounded-decision-right architecture under which the operational substrate is held. Document II.06 (Cognitive-Informational Disturbance Diagnostic) supplies the diagnostic substrate for the cognitive-and-informational dynamics under which the public-check architecture operates; the present document specifies the formal architecture under which the substrate is engaged.

Document XII.07 (Harm Response, Accountability, and Repair) operates in coordination with the present document on the harm-response institutions Thread D's public-trust threshold engages and on the constitutive-operations framing Thread E carries; the harm-response architecture and the public-check architecture operate together as ecosystem-and-habitat-scale cultivation conditions. Document II.09 (Climate Displacement and Belonging) operates in coordination with the present document on public-check rights at ecosystem-and-habitat scale bearing on displacement-driving decisions — decisions about regional resource extraction, regional infrastructure development, regional political-economic configuration — that the public-check architecture engages at the scale band the present document carries.

Document XIII.01 (Decision Rights Synthesis) carries forward the integration the four D-series documents together specify; the present document does not perform the cultivation-protocol synthesis but specifies the public-check decision rights at ecosystem-and-habitat scale under the four-element format. Document XIV.01 (Capstone Synthesis) reads the present document as the public-check specification within the broader Part-XII architecture and within the broader project arc.

The five public-check rights operate as cultivation conditions in the sense that the cultivation discipline the project's broader analytical apparatus articulates requires architectural commitments at the ecosystem-and-habitat scale band without which the cultivation work cannot proceed at finer scales. The e-democracy architecture is the cultivation condition under which population-level deliberation operates as ongoing public-check rather than as periodic-vote register; the technological-critique response is the cultivation condition under which technology-deployment is held to deliberative engagement before deployment forecloses the engagement; the public-trust threshold is the cultivation condition under which private-organization operation at this scale band operates under public-trust accountability; the bodily-and-reproductive-and-gender-autonomy public-check is the cultivation condition under which the constitutive-administrative operations are subject to deliberative engagement; the weapons-systems threading is the cultivation condition under which weapons-system development is engaged at the scales where the development occurs. The five together specify the cultivation conditions at the ecosystem-and-habitat scale band; the architecture proceeds.

The cultivation work proceeds.
