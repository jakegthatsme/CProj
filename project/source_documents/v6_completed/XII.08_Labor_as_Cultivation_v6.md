## PART ONE — LABOR AS CULTIVATION WORK AT INDIVIDUAL-AND-COMMUNITY SCALES

### 1.1 The cultivation framing

The cultivation framing reads labor as cultivation work operating at the individual scale of the person who works and at the community scale of the relationships and the material world that work cultivates. At the individual scale, labor is the activity through which the person develops capacities, skill, judgment, and the broader content of human-developmental work across the working life-course. At the community scale, labor is the activity through which communities cultivate their material conditions (food, shelter, the broader physical infrastructure of life), their social relationships (the cooperative practices through which collective work is conducted), and their cultural-cosmological inheritance (the practices through which cultural forms are transmitted across generations through work).

The framing operates against the labor-as-commodified-time framing the contemporary configuration has institutionalized. Under the commodified-time framing, labor is alienable hourly input bought and sold on a labor market, abstracted from the worker and from the activity, with the institutional apparatus organized around the wage relation as the principal mechanism through which work is structured. The framing produces specific structural-political-economic consequences: the alienation of the worker from the product of work (the worker does not own what the work produces), from the activity of work (the work is organized by external configurations rather than by the worker), from species-being (the work operates against the worker's capacity for self-cultivating activity), and from other workers (the labor-market apparatus operates through competitive rather than cooperative configurations).

### 1.2 The constitutional terrain

The argument that labor is constitutional terrain operates at three levels. First, the conditions under which the broader population conducts the work of their lives are constitutive of the conditions of life under the contemporary constitutional configuration. The contemporary American configuration's organization of labor — through the wage-labor configuration, the broader contemporary labor-market apparatus, the structural-political-economic alignment with the broader political-economic configuration — operates as the principal institutional architecture through which the conditions of contemporary American life are structured. Second, the contemporary configuration's structural-political-economic concentration produces specific labor-architectural consequences (the contemporary precariat configuration, the contemporary platform-economy configuration, the contemporary deskilling-and-automation configuration) that operate against the cultivation work labor under the cultivation framing supports. Third, the integration with the broader project commitments operates directly: the currency-architectural commitments `XII.02_Currency_Architecture_v6` engages, the e-democracy commitments `XII.03_E_Democracy_Structural_Public_Check_v6` engages, the intergenerational-rectification commitments `XII.04_Intergenerational_Rectification_v6` engages, the pedagogical-cultivation commitments `XII.06_Pedagogy_as_Constitutional_Cultivation_v6` engages, and the harm-response commitments `XII.07_Harm_Response_Accountability_Repair_v6` engages all operate jointly with the labor-cultivation commitments the present document develops.

### 1.3 The fifth-pass coverage gap

The fifth-pass arc's treatment of labor operated only on the negative side: the political-economic-disturbance diagnostic engaged labor displacement, automation, precarity, and the broader contemporary disturbance configuration without supplying the positive vision of work the constitutional cultivation work requires. The sixth-pass framing closes the coverage gap through the present document: `XII.08` supplies the positive vision of work the broader project commitments require, with the integration with the political-economic-disturbance diagnostic operating through cross-reference to `II.07_Political_Economic_Disturbance_Diagnostic_v6` rather than re-derivation.

## PART TWO — THE LABOR-AS-COMMODIFIED-TIME DIAGNOSTIC

### 2.1 Marx on labor-power, the working day, and alienation

Karl Marx's *Capital: A Critique of Political Economy*, Volume I (1867; English translation 1887), supplies the foundational analytical apparatus on the commodification of labor-power and on the structural-political-economic configuration of the wage relation. Marx's analytical contribution operates through three central commitments. The first is the analysis of labor-power as commodity — the structural-political-economic configuration in which the worker's capacity for work is sold as commodity on the labor market, with the structural-political-economic consequence that the worker is separated from the means of production and required to enter the wage relation as condition of subsistence. The second is the analysis of the working day — the chapters on the working day (Chapters 8-10 of Volume I) supplying the historical-empirical engagement with the contestation over the temporal length of the working day across the long industrial-capitalist period, with the structural-political-economic case that the working day operates as central site of class contestation. The third is the analysis of the labor process — the chapters on the labor process (Chapters 7, 13-15) supplying the analysis of the structural-political-economic configuration through which capital subordinates labor to its own organizing principles, with the structural-political-economic consequence that the worker is alienated from the activity of work.

Marx's *Economic and Philosophic Manuscripts of 1844* (published 1932) supplies the foundational analytical apparatus on alienated labor. Marx's analytical contribution operates through the articulation of four dimensions of alienation: the worker's estrangement from the product of work (the worker does not own what the work produces); from the activity of work (the work is organized by external configurations rather than by the worker); from species-being (the work operates against the worker's capacity for self-cultivating activity, with the philosophical-anthropological argument that human beings are species-being whose distinctive feature is the capacity for conscious productive activity); and from other workers (the labor-market apparatus operates through competitive rather than cooperative configurations).

The integration with the present argument is direct. Marx's analytical apparatus supplies the foundational diagnostic against which the cultivation framing operates: the commodification of labor-power, the contestation over the working day, the alienation of the worker from product, activity, species-being, and fellow workers operate as the structural-political-economic conditions that the cultivation framing engages.

### 2.2 Braverman on deskilling

Harry Braverman's *Labor and Monopoly Capital: The Degradation of Work in the Twentieth Century* (Monthly Review Press, 1974) supplies the contemporary analytical apparatus on the deskilling of work under the labor process of monopoly capital. Braverman's analytical contribution operates through the empirical-historical engagement with the labor process across the long twentieth-century period, with the structural-political-economic case that monopoly capital systematically separates conception from execution — the work of designing, planning, and coordinating production is concentrated in management and engineering apparatus, while the work of execution is systematically deskilled and reduced to repetitive task-fulfillment. The deskilling apparatus operates through the Taylorist scientific-management tradition Braverman engages and through the broader contemporary configurations including the contemporary office-and-platform-economy configurations.

The integration with the present argument operates through the analytical apparatus on the deskilling configuration: the cultivation framing's commitment to the cultivation of skill, judgment, and developmental capacity through work operates against the deskilling apparatus Braverman articulates, with the structural-political-economic implication that the cultivation work requires the reorganization of the labor process in ways that integrate conception with execution rather than systematically separating them.

### 2.3 What the commodified-time framing does

The labor-as-commodified-time framing operates as design-imposition framing: work is treated as alienable hourly input bought and sold on a labor market, abstracted from the worker and from the activity, with the structural-political-economic consequence that the worker is separated from the content of the work. The framing produces specific structural-political-economic consequences: the alienation across the four Marxian dimensions, the deskilling Braverman engages, the contemporary precariat configuration the broader contemporary labor-scholarship has documented, the contemporary platform-economy configuration's restructuring of the wage relation through algorithmic management, the contemporary financialization configuration's restructuring of capital-labor relations, and the broader content of the contemporary configuration's labor-architectural consequences.

### 2.4 What survives and what does not under the cultivation framing

Under the cultivation framing, the coordination of work, the genuine productivity of cooperation, and the content of skilled work survive: the cultivation framing engages these as constitutive of the labor-cultivation work. What does not survive is labor-power as commodity, time as the principal unit of work, the abstraction of work from the worker, the systematic deskilling apparatus, and the broader institutional architecture of the labor-market configuration. The cultivation framing reads coordination, cooperation, and skill not as outputs of the labor-market apparatus but as cultivation work that operates through different institutional architectures.

## PART FOUR — THE COOPERATIVE-ECONOMICS TRADITION

### 4.1 Mondragón Corporation

The Mondragón Corporation supplies the contemporary large-scale and decades-long instance of a federation of worker cooperatives. The Mondragón apparatus, established in 1956 in the Basque Country region of Spain by José María Arizmendiarrieta and operating in its contemporary form as the Mondragón Corporación Cooperativa, includes approximately ninety-five worker-cooperatives across multiple economic sectors with collective employment of approximately seventy thousand workers. The Mondragón institutional architecture includes one-worker-one-vote governance within each cooperative; worker-ownership configuration in which workers own the cooperative collectively and the broader corporate-cooperative structure protects against subsequent demutualization; wage-compression configuration in which the wage ratio between the highest-paid and lowest-paid workers within each cooperative is institutionally constrained; and the broader institutional architecture across the corporation including the Caja Laboral (cooperative bank), Mondragón University, the research-and-development infrastructure, and the broader social-cooperative-institutional architecture.

The Mondragón apparatus has produced an operational record across nearly seven decades that articulates the substantive feasibility of worker-cooperative architecture at scale across multiple economic sectors and across multiple decades of political-economic transformation. The integration with the present argument operates at primary-articulator standing within the cooperative-economics tradition. The contemporary scholarly literature including the work of Sharryn Kasmir (*The Myth of Mondragón*, 1996), George Cheney (*Values at Work: Employee Participation Meets Market Pressure at Mondragón*, 1999), and the broader contemporary Mondragón scholarship supplies the contemporary articulation, with the critical engagement on the operational difficulties Mondragón has encountered (the contemporary tensions between the cooperative-architectural commitments and the conditions of contemporary global capitalism) supplying institutional-design vocabulary the contemporary cultivation work engages.

### 4.2 Cooperation Jackson

Cooperation Jackson, established in 2014 in Jackson, Mississippi and articulated through Kali Akuno and Ajamu Nangwaya's edited *Jackson Rising: The Struggle for Economic Democracy and Black Self-Determination in Jackson, Mississippi* (Daraja Press, 2017), supplies the contemporary American articulation of solidarity-economy cooperative work rooted in the Black-radical tradition. The Cooperation Jackson apparatus operates through the cultivation of worker cooperatives across multiple economic sectors in Jackson, the broader political-architectural commitments to the cultivation of solidarity-economy infrastructure across the Jackson urban configuration, and the integration with the broader Black-radical-political-architectural commitments the Jackson tradition has cultivated across the post-civil-rights period. The Akuno-Nangwaya volume supplies the contemporary documentation of the Cooperation Jackson apparatus and articulates the institutional-architectural commitments the work has cultivated.

### 4.3 Marjorie Kelly on generative versus extractive ownership

Marjorie Kelly's *Owning Our Future: The Emerging Ownership Revolution* (Berrett-Koehler, 2012) supplies the contemporary analytical apparatus on the distinction between extractive and generative ownership design. Kelly's analytical contribution operates through the structural-political-economic engagement with ownership architecture: extractive ownership operates through the configuration in which ownership is structured for the extraction of value from the enterprise to the owners (the contemporary publicly-traded-corporation configuration as paradigmatic instance), while generative ownership operates through configurations in which ownership is structured for the cultivation of the enterprise's operation and its contribution to broader configurations (worker cooperatives, employee-owned firms, public-benefit corporations, community-owned enterprises, and the broader content of generative-ownership architectures). Kelly's *The Making of a Democratic Economy: How to Build Prosperity for the Many, Not the Few* (with Ted Howard, Berrett-Koehler, 2019) extends the analytical apparatus.

### 4.4 What the cooperative-economics tradition establishes

The cooperative-economics tradition establishes for the cultivation framing that worker ownership and democratic enterprise are not utopian propositions but operating realities across multiple jurisdictions and across multiple decades, and that the ownership architecture of an enterprise determines whether work is cultivation or extraction. The institutional commitments engage the cultivation of cooperative-and-worker-owned enterprise architectures, the structural-political-economic alignment with the broader currency-architectural commitments `XII.02_Currency_Architecture_v6` engages, and the broader institutional-architectural reconstruction the cooperative-economics tradition supports.

## PART FIVE — THE WORKPLACE-DEMOCRACY TRADITION

### 5.1 Carole Pateman on participation in the workplace

Carole Pateman's *Participation and Democratic Theory* (Cambridge University Press, 1970) supplies the foundational contemporary articulation of workplace democracy as constitutive of participatory political theory. Pateman's analytical contribution operates through the structural-political-philosophical engagement with the democratic theory of the post-war period — the dominant democratic theory had confined democracy to the political sphere and treated the workplace as outside democratic terrain — and through the analytical case that the workplace is constitutive site of democratic practice. Pateman's argument operates at two levels: the workplace is itself a site of governance that warrants democratic organization on the same grounds as the broader political sphere, and the workplace operates as the training ground and the precondition of participatory political-cultural practice (the experience of democratic practice in the workplace cultivates the dispositions and capacities the broader political-cultural participatory practice requires).

### 5.2 Robert Dahl on economic democracy

Robert Dahl's *A Preface to Economic Democracy* (University of California Press, 1985) supplies the contemporary articulation of economic democracy. Dahl's analytical contribution operates through the structural-political-philosophical argument that the reasons that justify democracy in the state justify it in the firm: if the case for democratic governance in the political sphere is the case that those subjected to governance ought to participate in it, the parallel case operates for the firm, in which workers are subjected to substantial governance authority on a daily basis across the working life-course. Dahl's argument articulates the structural-political-philosophical foundation for the workplace-democracy commitment the cultivation framing develops.

### 5.3 What the workplace-democracy tradition establishes

The workplace-democracy tradition establishes for the cultivation framing that the firm is a site of governance that warrants democratic organization, that the exclusion of democracy from the firm operates as choice rather than necessity, and that the e-democracy feedback architecture `XII.03_E_Democracy_Structural_Public_Check_v6` develops bears on workplace governance. The integration with the e-democracy commitments operates through the structural-architectural commitment that workplace governance operates as one specific scale at which the e-democracy feedback architecture is operationalized.

## PART SIX — THE POST-WORK TRADITION

### 6.1 André Gorz on the critique of the work-based society

André Gorz's *Farewell to the Working Class* (South End Press, 1980; French original *Adieux au prolétariat*, 1980) and *Critique of Economic Reason* (Verso, 1989; French original 1988) supply the foundational contemporary articulation of the post-work tradition. Gorz's analytical contribution operates through the structural-political-philosophical critique of the work-based society — the contemporary configuration in which the social-cultural-political identity of persons is centrally constituted through their relation to wage labor — and the articulation of the reduction of heteronomous work (work conducted under conditions external to the worker's substantive cultivation work) as constitutive of political-cultural reconstruction. Gorz's analytical case is that the contemporary configuration's productive capacity has reached the point where the work-based society is no longer required for the production of material conditions, and that the cultivation of post-work configurations is consequently politically-architecturally available.

### 6.3 Guy Standing on the precariat and basic income

### 6.4 David Graeber on bullshit jobs and debt

Graeber's analytical contribution operates through the empirical-anthropological engagement with the contemporary configuration's production of work whose content the workers themselves regard as meaningless, with the structural-political-cultural analysis of why the contemporary configuration produces this work in substantial volume and what the work-architectural implications are. The post-work tradition contributes the recognition that not all that is called work is cultivation work, and that the cultivation framing must include the freedom not to work as well as the conditions of good work. ### 9.3 What the disability-justice tradition articulates

The disability-justice tradition articulates that "labor as cultivation" must not become a new norm against which disabled people who do not or cannot do waged work are measured, and that the cultivation framing must hold the right not to work alongside the conditions of good work. The structural-political-cultural commitment operates against the dignity-of-work tradition's potential for the cultivation of a new normative configuration in which disabled persons whose bodyminds do not engage waged-labor configuration are systematically marginalized within the cultivation framing itself. The integration with the post-work tradition Part Six engaged operates through the joint cultivation commitments to the freedom not to work and to substantive livelihood configurations decoupled from waged labor.

The integration with `XII.05_Neurodivergent_Polis_v6`'s disability-justice and Mad-studies engagement, with `VI.07_Care_Kinship_Social_Reproduction_v6`'s care-and-social-reproduction cultivation, and with `XII.02_Currency_Architecture_v6`'s currency-architectural commitments to substantive provision-decoupled-from-waged-labor operates as constitutive of the cultivation work.

## PART TEN — THE CULTIVATION SPECIFICATION AND THE BRIDGE TO THE DECISION-RIGHTS ARCHITECTURE

### 10.1 What the constitutional architecture must enable

The cultivation framing specifies four labor-cultivation conditions the constitutional architecture must enable. First, democratic and cooperative ownership operate as constitutional option. The institutional architecture supports the cultivation of worker-cooperative, employee-owned, public-benefit-corporation, and broader generative-ownership configurations through structural-political-economic commitments including the cooperative-financing infrastructure (cooperative-banking institutions integrated with the public-currency channel `XII.02_Currency_Architecture_v6` engages), the cooperative-development infrastructure (technical-assistance, training, and broader cultivation-support apparatus), the legal-architectural commitments to cooperative-form legal-protection across multiple jurisdictional levels, and the broader institutional-architectural reconstruction.

Second, workplace governance operates as site of the participatory polity. The institutional architecture engages the workplace as one of the principal sites at which the e-democracy feedback architecture `XII.03_E_Democracy_Structural_Public_Check_v6` is operationalized, with the structural commitments engaging worker participation in workplace governance, the broader cultivation of workplace-democratic practice, and the integration with the broader participatory-political-cultural commitments the project supports.

Third, livelihood is decoupled from waged labor where the post-work and disability-justice traditions require it. The institutional architecture supports basic-income, public-employment, and broader livelihood-architectural configurations through which livelihood operates outside the waged-labor configuration. The integration with `XII.02_Currency_Architecture_v6`'s public-currency channel and with `XII.11_Provisioning_Decision_Rights_v6`'s provisioning decision rights operates at the institutional-architectural level.

Fourth, work operates as reciprocity rather than as extraction. The institutional architecture engages the Indigenous labor-as-relational tradition's commitments at the level of land-relational work, ecological-restoration work, food-and-agriculture work `VI.02_Food_Soil_Agriculture_v6` engages, built-environment work `VI.05_Regenerative_Built_Environment_v6` engages, care work `VI.07_Care_Kinship_Social_Reproduction_v6` engages, and the broader content of work that operates as participation in reciprocal relational configurations rather than as commodity-form extraction.

### 10.2 The bridge to the decision-rights architecture

The cultivation specification bridges to the currency, e-democracy, and provisioning architectures. `XII.02_Currency_Architecture_v6`'s unidirectional dual-currency architecture supplies the operational apparatus for livelihood configurations decoupled from waged labor; `XII.03_E_Democracy_Structural_Public_Check_v6`'s e-democracy feedback architecture supplies the operational apparatus for workplace-governance commitments; `XII.11_Provisioning_Decision_Rights_v6`'s provisioning decision rights supply the formal bounded-decision-right specification at the provisioning scale. The integration with `XIII.01_Decision_Rights_Synthesis_v6`'s cultivation-protocol synthesis operates through the labor-cultivation commitments the synthesis integrates with the broader constitutional commitments. The integration with `XIV.01_Capstone_Synthesis_v6` operates through the capstone reading of labor-cultivation as one of the load-bearing structural commitments of the v6 corpus. The integration with `VI.05_Regenerative_Built_Environment_v6` (built environment as labor terrain), `VI.02_Food_Soil_Agriculture_v6` (food and agriculture as labor terrain), and `VI.07_Care_Kinship_Social_Reproduction_v6` (care work as labor) operates at the material-substrate-cultivation level.

### 10.3 Closing — labor as cultivation work

Labor operates as cultivation work the polity conducts through the practices through which persons and communities cultivate themselves, their relationships, and their material world. The cultivation work is constitutional because the conditions of work are the conditions of a cultivated or an alienated life. The cultivation work is multi-traditional: the Indigenous labor-as-relational tradition, the Black-radical labor tradition, the disability-justice critique of labor, the cooperative-economics tradition, the workplace-democracy tradition, and the post-work tradition stand at primary-articulator standing alongside the dignity-of-work tradition. The cultivation work closes the fifth-pass coverage gap and supplies the positive vision of work the broader project commitments require.

The cultivation work proceeds.
