## Thread B — Unidirectional Dual Currency Convertibility as Foundational Decision Right

The unidirectional dual currency convertibility — operating between a political-economic currency and an ecological currency — is specified as a foundational decision right with the four-element format. Document XII.02 (Currency Architecture) articulates the dual-currency apparatus itself at operational depth: the political-economic currency operating in the conventional money-and-credit register; the ecological currency operating as denomination of ecological standing under the biocultural-governance ontology; the convertibility relation between them, with the unidirectional commitment specifying that conversion proceeds from political-economic into ecological denomination without reverse conversion at any scale at which biospheric impact is at stake. The present document specifies the formal bounded-decision-right architecture that conditions the dual-currency apparatus as foundational right rather than as engineerable institutional choice.

**Scope.** The right operates over the convertibility relation between the political-economic currency and the ecological currency. The convertibility relation is the load-bearing architectural commitment in the dual-currency apparatus; the unidirectional specification governs which direction the convertibility runs and under what conditions. The scope is not the dual-currency apparatus itself (Document XII.02's terrain) but the meta-commitment governing the apparatus's operational substrate — the commitment that political-economic decisions cannot dissolve ecological standing through monetary substitution at any scale at which biospheric impact is at stake. The scope is bounded: the right does not specify the substantive content of the ecological-currency denomination (Document XII.02's terrain), nor the cross-scale ecological-currency dynamics (Documents V.01 and X.01's terrain), nor the operational integration with the political-economy architecture (Document X.01's Political Economy of Transformative Legislation specifies). The right specifies only the convertibility-relation meta-commitment.

**Scale.** Biosphere-and-ecozone scale. The right operates at the scale at which political-economic decisions have aggregate-biospheric reach: monetary architectures whose contraction or expansion bears on aggregate carbon-cycle, hydrological-cycle, nitrogen-cycle, and broader biospheric dynamics. The scale-matching specifies that the unidirectional-convertibility commitment is held at the scale band where the architectural commitment makes operational sense — biospheric and continental-scale ecozone dynamics — and that finer-scale ecological dynamics are conditioned by the cross-scale coupling specifications Documents XII.10 and XII.11 articulate at ecosystem-and-habitat and habitat-and-population scales. The biosphere-and-ecozone-scale specification draws on the nested-scales reference frame Document V.01 articulates: the biospheric scale operates across the longest, slowest cycles; the ecozone scale operates within continental and oceanic-basin spatial extents; the rights at this scale band are the foundational rights without which the cross-scale architecture cannot hold.

**Feedback condition.** The dual-currency mechanism is itself feedback architecture, in the sense Document XII.02 articulates: the cross-currency translation operates as ongoing signal through which political-economic and ecological dynamics are coupled at constitutive depth rather than at supplementary register. The unidirectional commitment is the feedback architecture's structural commitment — the architecture cannot operate as cultivation condition if the convertibility is bidirectional, because bidirectional convertibility re-establishes the substitution dynamic the architecture is drafted to prevent. Beyond the cross-currency translation, the right's feedback condition specifies ongoing ecological monitoring in the eco-mimetic-monitoring register Document XII.01's Thread G articulates: continuous monitoring of biospheric performance across the scale band, with diverse-epistemic-standing monitoring (Indigenous monitoring institutions; community-based monitoring; technical-scientific monitoring; deliberative-public monitoring) operating at parity. The feedback condition is not the right's operation through monitoring but the architecture by which the right's performance under the ecological dynamics is sensed continuously across scales. Where the monitoring detects systemic erosion of the unidirectional commitment — incipient bidirectionality, leakage, scope-of-application contraction — the feedback condition triggers the review architecture Document XII.01's Thread G specifies, with the multi-disposition outputs that architecture authorizes.

**Sunset-or-adaptation condition.** The convertibility-relation specification is subject to scheduled review at the temporal scale appropriate to biospheric-and-ecozone dynamics. The temporal scale is not the political-electoral cycle the contemporary American political architecture operates within; the dynamics the right governs operate across decades and centuries, and the review cycle must match those dynamics rather than the electoral cycle the legislative apparatus prefers. The sunset-or-adaptation condition specifies that the convertibility-relation meta-commitment is reviewed under the adaptive-cycle phase apparatus Document V.01 carries: under conservation-phase conditions the convertibility specification is stabilized with codification work; under release-phase conditions where the architecture's biospheric performance has deteriorated, the convertibility specification can be reviewed under the panarchy framework Document X.01's Political Economy of Transformative Legislation articulates. The sunset-or-adaptation condition does not specify automatic temporal sunset (the commitment is foundational and cannot be casually allowed to lapse); it specifies the conditions under which scheduled review and amendment are appropriate, coordinated with the reversible-amendment-process right Thread C specifies.

The right's operational specification draws on the comparative-monetary-economics literature where it has begun to engage the architectural commitments dual-currency systems require: Joseph Huber's *Sovereign Money: Beyond Reserve Banking* (2017) on the architectural commitments money-creation governance carries; Mary Mellor's *The Future of Money: From Financial Crisis to Public Resource* (2010) on money-as-public-resource; the broader heterodox-monetary-economics tradition through Hyman Minsky's *Stabilizing an Unstable Economy* (1986) and Stephanie Kelton's *The Deficit Myth: Modern Monetary Theory and the Birth of the People's Economy* (2020). The right also draws on the ecological-economics tradition through Herman Daly's *Beyond Growth: The Economics of Sustainable Development* (1996) and Robert Costanza et al.'s ecological-economics corpus, on the ecological-currency-denomination side of the convertibility relation. Document XII.02 carries the engagement with these literatures; the present document specifies only the formal bounded-decision-right architecture conditioning the apparatus.

## Thread C — Reversible Amendment Process as Foundational Decision Right

The reversible amendment process is specified as a foundational decision right with the four-element format. The right operates over the meta-decision of how the constitutional architecture is itself revised, with reversibility specifying that no amendment is engineered-fixture revision but cultivation-condition adjustment under specified feedback architecture and sunset-or-adaptation conditions.

**Scope.** The right operates over the meta-decision of how the constitutional architecture is itself revised. The scope is the meta-architectural commitment that no amendment is final, that every amendment carries explicit provision for revision under specified conditions, and that the architecture is cultivated rather than designed-as-final-fixture. The scope is bounded: the right does not specify the content of any particular amendment, nor the political process by which amendments are proposed and adopted (the e-democracy-as-structural-public-check architecture Document XII.03 articulates carries that work), nor the substantive amendments the project's broader architectural commitments imply. The right specifies only the reversibility meta-commitment as foundational architectural condition.

**Scale.** Biosphere-and-ecozone, where the meta-decision affects the architecture's capacity to register and respond to biospheric-scale disturbances. The reversible-amendment specification holds at the foundational scale because the substantive commitments at finer scales (ecosystem-and-habitat in XII.10, habitat-and-population in XII.11, organism-and-organ-system with intergenerational coupling in XII.12) cannot be cultivated under conditions where the meta-architecture is treated as engineered fixture. The cross-scale coupling specifies that the reversibility commitment at biosphere-and-ecozone scale conditions the operational capacity at finer scales; the foundational reversibility is what enables the finer-scale architectural commitments to operate as cultivation conditions rather than as fixed-engineered specifications.

**Feedback condition.** Amendment-and-reversal feedback operates through review architecture coordinated with Document XII.03 (E-Democracy and the Structural Public Check). The structural-public-check architecture Document XII.03 articulates carries the deliberative substrate through which the population participates in the amendment process at substantive deliberative depth rather than through the thin participation-as-vote register the contemporary American constitutional architecture operates within. The feedback condition specifies that amendment proposals are subject to deliberative engagement at the scale band the amendment operates at, with the cross-scale-feedback specifications the digital-deliberation infrastructure Document XII.03 articulates carries. The feedback condition further specifies that proposed amendments include explicit reversibility specifications — what conditions would trigger reversal, what review architecture would conduct the reversal, what cross-scale-coupling considerations the reversal must accommodate — drafted into the amendment text rather than left for later determination.

The comparative-constitutional-amendment literature has begun to articulate the architectural commitments reversible amendment requires, though the literature has not yet articulated the eco-mimetic cultivation-discipline framing the present document operates under. Richard Albert's *Constitutional Amendments: Making, Breaking, and Changing Constitutions* (Oxford University Press, 2019) supplies analytical apparatus distinguishing the various forms of constitutional revision — formal amendment, informal amendment, constitutional dismemberment, and constitutional replacement — and articulates the conditions under which each operates as architectural-revision practice. Albert's analytical work positions the amendment process as substantive architectural commitment rather than as procedural technicality, with attention to the comparative-constitutional record across multiple jurisdictions. Sanford Levinson's *Our Undemocratic Constitution: Where the Constitution Goes Wrong (and How We the People Can Correct It)* (Oxford University Press, 2006) articulates the architectural-democratic implications of the contemporary American amendment apparatus — Article V's near-impossibility threshold for formal amendment, the resulting reliance on judicial reinterpretation as informal amendment, the resulting democratic deficit. The reversible-amendment specification the present document carries draws on both literatures while operating under the cultivation-discipline framing the project's biocultural-governance ontology and eco-mimetic policy-method articulate.

**Sunset-or-adaptation condition.** Each amendment carries explicit sunset or scheduled-review specification, with the temporal scale of the sunset matched to the scale band the amendment operates at. Amendments operating at biosphere-and-ecozone-scale dynamics carry review cycles measured in decades and centuries; amendments operating at ecosystem-and-habitat-scale dynamics carry review cycles measured in years and decades; amendments operating at finer scales carry correspondingly shorter cycles. The temporal scale-matching specifies that the sunset-or-adaptation condition is not a uniform calendar but an apparatus drafted into each amendment under the scale-matching specification the present document's four-element format establishes. The sunset-or-adaptation condition coordinates with the legislative-cycle architecture Document XII.01's Thread E articulates: review under r-phase conditions, codification under K-phase conditions, managed release under Ω-phase conditions, reorganization under α-phase conditions.

## Thread D — Judicial Review of Executive Orders as Foundational Decision Right

The judicial review of executive orders is specified as a foundational decision right with the four-element format. The right operates over executive-order outputs as decisions reviewable against the foundational normative commitments Document I.01 specifies, and reads judicial review in the eco-mimetic-feedback register Document XII.01 articulates rather than in the terminal-adjudication register the contemporary American constitutional architecture treats as default.

**Scope.** The right operates over executive-order outputs as decisions reviewable against the Document I.01 foundational normative commitments. The scope specifies that executive decisions — not statutory law (Congress's terrain), not judicial decisions (the court system's terrain), but the unilateral-executive-decision register that has accumulated extra-constitutional reach across the contemporary American constitutional record — are subject to ongoing review against the foundational normative commitments rather than treated as final unless overturned by extraordinary judicial intervention. The scope is bounded: the right does not specify the content of any particular executive decision, nor the substantive content of the judicial review's outputs, nor the broader executive-power apparatus the comparative-constitutional record engages. The right specifies the foundational architectural commitment that executive decisions are reviewable as feedback channel rather than as terminal-decision channel.

**Scale.** Biosphere-and-ecozone, where executive decisions have aggregate-impact reach beyond the decision-right scope to which executive orders are formally addressed. The scale-matching specifies that executive orders bearing on biosphere-and-ecozone-scale dynamics — climate-policy executive orders, military-and-defense executive orders with environmental reach, trade-policy executive orders with cross-continental supply-chain reach, public-health executive orders with population-scale reach — are subject to the scale-matched feedback architecture the right specifies. Executive orders bearing on finer-scale dynamics are conditioned by the rights at the finer scale bands (XII.10 through XII.12).

**Feedback condition.** Judicial review is read as feedback architecture in the eco-mimetic sense Document XII.01's Thread G articulates: ongoing cross-scale sensing of executive-decision performance against the foundational normative commitments, with diverse-epistemic-standing monitoring at parity, and with multi-disposition outputs (not just uphold-or-strike but also amendment-trigger, adaptation-trigger, succession-trigger, panarchy-cycle-trigger). The judicial-review-as-feedback specification departs from the contemporary American Article III review architecture, which operates at one institutional scale (the federal court hierarchy), with discrete cases as the trigger and binary uphold/strike outputs. The right specifies that judicial review of executive orders operates as continuous monitoring across scales, with case-triggered review supplemented by standing-monitoring-institution review at the biosphere-and-ecozone scale band the right operates at. The standing monitoring institutions include Indigenous monitoring institutions (the Haudenosaunee Grand Council's standing capacity to monitor executive-decision impact on treaty commitments, for instance), community-based monitoring infrastructures, technical-scientific monitoring through the federal scientific apparatus the Articles establish, and deliberative-public monitoring through the Document XII.03 e-democracy architecture.

The comparative-constitutional-judicial-review literature has begun to articulate the architectural commitments weak-form judicial review requires. Mark Tushnet's *Weak Courts, Strong Rights: Judicial Review and Social Welfare Rights in Comparative Constitutional Law* (Princeton University Press, 2008) articulates weak-form judicial review as one architectural alternative to the strong-form American Article III apparatus: weak-form review operates with the court flagging constitutional concerns without final-veto power, with the legislative branch retaining final-decision authority subject to ongoing review. Stephen Gardbaum's *The New Commonwealth Model of Constitutionalism: Theory and Practice* (Cambridge University Press, 2013) extends the analytical work to the Commonwealth-model jurisdictions (the United Kingdom under the Human Rights Act 1998, Canada under the Charter, New Zealand under the Bill of Rights Act, Australia under the Victorian and ACT human-rights legislation). The transformative-constitutionalism literature, through Karl Klare's "Legal Culture and Transformative Constitutionalism" (*South African Journal on Human Rights* 14, no. 1, 1998) and Upendra Baxi's *The Future of Human Rights* (Oxford University Press India, 2002), articulates the architectural commitments transformative-constitutional review requires: the South African post-apartheid Constitutional Court's transformative jurisprudence, the Indian Supreme Court's public-interest-litigation tradition, the broader transformative-constitutionalism record across multiple jurisdictions. The judicial-review-of-executive-orders right the present document specifies draws on both the weak-form-review and transformative-constitutionalism literatures while operating under the eco-mimetic-feedback framing Document XII.01 establishes.

**Sunset-or-adaptation condition.** Judicial-review outcomes are themselves subject to scheduled review and to the reversible-amendment process Thread C specifies. The sunset-or-adaptation condition specifies that no judicial-review output is final-engineered-fixture; every output carries explicit provision for review under specified conditions, with the temporal scale matched to the dynamics the executive order operated at. The cross-scale coupling specifies that judicial-review outputs at biosphere-and-ecozone scale operate as constitutional-architectural commitments subject to the reversibility specification Thread C carries; the outputs are not terminal adjudications but cultivation-condition adjustments under the broader architectural cycle.

## Thread E — Sexuality, Gender, and Reproduction: Bodily and Reproductive Autonomy as Foundational Decision Rights (Sixth-Pass Addition)

The Western political-theory canon presupposed a sexual contract subordinating women that the dominant social-contract literature substantially concealed; Carole Pateman's *The Sexual Contract* (Stanford University Press, 1988) carries the foundational articulation of this concealment as the social-contract tradition's constitutive operation. The implication for the constitutional-architectural commitments the foundational decision rights operate under is that the social-contract apparatus the dominant constitutional tradition inherits cannot be treated as adequate substrate for the foundational rights at biospheric scale without engagement with the sexual contract Pateman's analytical apparatus identifies. Catharine A. ## Thread F — Defense, Military, and War Powers, Threaded Through Judicial Review of Executive Orders (Sixth-Pass Addition)

The sixth-pass arc adds the defense-and-war-powers thread at scope-out-with-threading standing: the project treats war and defense as defensible scope-out from the cultivation framework's center of gravity (which operates substantially at the biocultural-governance, eco-mimetic-policy-making, and disturbance-adapted-implementation registers rather than at the war-and-defense register), threaded through the documents where the war-and-defense question bears on the architecture rather than given standalone home. Document XII.09 carries the thread through the judicial-review-of-executive-orders right of Thread D: executive war-making operates as the paradigm case of an executive decision with biospheric-scale reach that the scale-matched feedback architecture must check.

The constitutional history of war powers articulates a substantive architectural commitment under the original Constitution that the contemporary configuration has substantially eroded. Louis Fisher's *Presidential War Power* (University Press of Kansas, 1995, and later revised editions) carries the foundational analytical apparatus on the constitutional allocation of the war decision. Fisher's analytical work specifies that the original constitutional commitment vested the war-declaration authority in Congress (Article I, Section 8, Clause 11) and the commander-in-chief authority in the President (Article II, Section 2), with the analytical-architectural commitment that the decision to initiate war operated as legislative-deliberative decision and the decision to conduct ongoing military operations under congressional war declaration operated as executive-operational decision. The original architectural commitment is identifiable through the Convention debates (Madison's notes on the August 17, 1787 debate over the war-declaration clause, in which the Convention shifted the verb from "make war" to "declare war" specifically to clarify that the executive retained authority to respond to attack but not to initiate war), through *The Federalist* No. 69 (in which Hamilton specified that the commander-in-chief authority was nothing more than "first general and admiral" authority and was distinct from the British Crown's prerogative to declare war), and through the broader Founding-era record. The architectural commitment has been substantially eroded across the twentieth and twenty-first centuries through the Korean War (initiated without congressional declaration in 1950), the Vietnam War (escalated under Tonkin Gulf Resolution without subsequent congressional declaration), the multiple post-1973 deployments under the War Powers Resolution that have operated under executive interpretation rather than under congressional control, and the post-September-11 Authorizations for Use of Military Force that have been interpreted as authorizing ongoing operations across multiple decades and multiple geographic theaters with substantial executive discretion.

John Hart Ely's *War and Responsibility: Constitutional Lessons of Vietnam and Its Aftermath* (Princeton University Press, 1993) extends the analytical work into the War Powers Resolution of 1973 and into the architectural commitments the resolution attempted to articulate. Ely's analytical work specifies that the War Powers Resolution operated under analytical commitments that the contemporary executive interpretation has substantially undermined, with the resolution's reporting requirements and the sixty-day-and-thirty-day clocks operating as architectural commitments that successive administrations have either evaded or interpreted in ways that substantially negate the resolution's operational substance. The architectural implication for the judicial-review-of-executive-orders right is that executive war-making operates as the paradigm case of executive decisions with biospheric-scale reach that the scale-matched feedback architecture must check — and that the contemporary American constitutional architecture has substantially failed to maintain the architectural commitments the original constitutional allocation specified. Mary L. Dudziak's *War Time: An Idea, Its History, Its Consequences* (Oxford University Press, 2012) extends the analytical work into the temporal-architectural register: "wartime" as analytical category has been made permanent across the post-1945 period, with the architectural implication that the temporal commitments the Constitution's war-powers allocation presupposed (a distinction between wartime and peacetime, with substantively different operational architectures across the two) have substantially collapsed under contemporary practice. The architectural implication for the judicial-review-of-executive-orders right is that executive decisions across the broader national-security register cannot be reviewed under the assumption that wartime is exceptional condition; the analytical work calls for review under the recognition that the wartime-as-permanent condition is the contemporary architectural baseline.

*The Federalist* No. 69 (Hamilton, March 14, 1788) on the commander-in-chief clause specifies that the executive's military authority is substantially narrower than the British Crown's prerogative — limited to "supreme command and direction of the military and naval forces" without authority to declare war or to raise armies, which authorities are vested in Congress. *The Federalist* No. 74 (Hamilton, March 25, 1788) on the executive's military authority articulates the analytical work supporting the unitary-executive command structure during operations Congress has authorized, while preserving the architectural distinction between operations-conduct authority and operations-initiation authority. The two papers are read against the Anti-Federalist concerns about standing armies that animated the Convention's debates and the early ratification controversies — concerns articulated through Centinel, Brutus, and the broader Anti-Federalist corpus (cross-referenced to Document I.03 Federalist/Anti-Federalist Records). The reading specifies that the original constitutional architecture operated under concern about executive war-making as architectural risk, with the commander-in-chief clause drafted under that concern rather than as authorization for executive war-making at the scale the contemporary configuration permits.

Hannah Arendt's *On Violence* (Harcourt, 1970) carries analytical work on the relationship between power and violence that bears on the war-powers question at analytical depth. Arendt distinguishes power (the human capacity to act in concert) from violence (the instrumental application of force) and articulates that the two operate in inverse relation: power is constituted through concerted action and erodes when the action is displaced by instrumental violence; violence substitutes for power when power has eroded. The architectural implication for the judicial-review-of-executive-orders right at the war-powers register is that executive war-making operates as substitution of violence for power — the executive's substantial reliance on military operations as architectural method substitutes for the concerted-action register the constitutional architecture's deliberative apparatus is drafted to operate within. The analytical work positions the war-powers question as substantive architectural commitment with implications across the broader constitutional configuration rather than as narrow procedural question about the allocation of authority between Congress and the executive.

Michael Walzer's *Just and Unjust Wars: A Moral Argument with Historical Illustrations* (Basic Books, 1977) supplies analytical apparatus on the just-war tradition that the analytical work engages critically. Walzer's analytical work articulates the just-war tradition's substantive content — jus ad bellum (the conditions under which war is justly initiated), jus in bello (the conditions under which war is justly conducted), with later additions of jus post bellum (the conditions under which war is justly concluded). The architectural implication for the judicial-review-of-executive-orders right is that the just-war tradition supplies analytical resources for reviewing executive war-making decisions against normative criteria rather than against formal procedural-allocation criteria alone. The critical engagement specifies that the just-war tradition operates substantively under the assumption that war is a defensible architectural commitment under specified conditions — an assumption the project's broader engagement with the pacifist tradition treats as itself subject to architectural review.

The pacifist tradition supplies analytical apparatus that the just-war tradition cannot fully accommodate. The historic-peace-church Anabaptist political theology of conscience — engaged through its analytical work rather than through individual scholars whose biographical record disqualifies their personal citation — carries the foundational analytical articulation that war is itself architectural commitment rather than emergency exception to ordinary architectural commitments. The analytical work specifies that the Anabaptist political-theological tradition operated under the architectural commitment that war participation conflicts with the substantive commitments the tradition articulates, with the implication that the architecture of conscience-exemption operated as constitutional architecture in the post-Reformation European jurisdictions and in the early American republic. The architectural implication for the foundational decision rights is that the conscience-exemption architecture operates as foundational architectural commitment at biospheric scale, with implications for the judicial-review-of-executive-orders right and for the broader Part-XII architecture. The engagement proceeds through the analytical work rather than through the personal citation of individual scholars whose biographical record disqualifies them.

Mohandas K. Gandhi's *Hind Swaraj* (1909) carries the foundational analytical articulation of the *ahimsa* (non-violence) tradition as architectural-political commitment. Gandhi's analytical work specifies that *ahimsa* operates not as supplementary moral principle attached to ordinary political architecture but as substantive architectural commitment from which the broader political architecture proceeds. The analytical work positions non-violence as foundational architectural commitment with implications across the broader political-architectural configuration — implications for economic architecture (the *swadeshi* commitment to local production), for legal architecture (the architectural commitments under which conscience-based resistance to unjust law operates), for political-deliberative architecture (the architectural commitments under which collective decisions are reached without coercion). The architectural implication for the judicial-review-of-executive-orders right at the war-powers register is that the *ahimsa* tradition carries primary-articulator analytical resources for reviewing executive war-making against substantive non-violent-architectural commitments.

Roxanne Dunbar-Ortiz's *An Indigenous Peoples' History of the United States* (Beacon Press, 2014) carries the foundational analytical articulation of the United States military's role in Indigenous dispossession across the continental and oceanic United States. Dunbar-Ortiz's analytical work specifies that the United States military operated as the principal architectural instrument of Indigenous dispossession from the eighteenth century forward, with the military's substantive content across multiple campaigns — the Indian Removal Act and its execution through military force in the 1830s, the post-Civil-War military campaigns against the Plains nations, the Pacific Northwest campaigns, the campaigns in Alaska and the Pacific, the contemporary military presence on Indigenous lands — operating as architectural commitment to dispossession across the constitutional record. The architectural implication for the judicial-review-of-executive-orders right at the war-powers register is that executive war-making cannot be reviewed only against the formal-procedural-allocation criteria the dominant constitutional discourse engages; the analytical work calls for review against the substantive architectural-dispossession criteria the Indigenous-historical record establishes. Nick Estes (Lower Brule Sioux), *Our History Is the Future: Standing Rock Versus the Dakota Access Pipeline, and the Long Tradition of Indigenous Resistance* (Verso, 2019), extends the analytical work into the contemporary register: the Standing Rock resistance to the Dakota Access Pipeline operated under architectural conditions in which the United States military and the broader security apparatus operated against the Indigenous-architectural commitments the resistance articulated. The architectural implication is that the judicial-review-of-executive-orders right at the war-powers register engages not only the foreign-military-operations register the dominant war-powers discourse engages but also the domestic-security-operations register that the contemporary configuration substantially incorporates.

The World Beyond War documentation — the public-record materials on the substantive architectural commitments the contemporary American military operates under, the analytical work the abolitionist-anti-war movement has carried since the founding of the contemporary American peace movement in the post-Vietnam decades, and the contemporary documentation of the military's biospheric impact — supplies analytical resource on the contemporary architectural commitments the military operates under. The architectural implication for the judicial-review-of-executive-orders right at the war-powers register is that executive war-making operates as biosphere-and-ecozone-scale architectural commitment with substantial environmental reach beyond the war-powers question's narrower formal scope.

The right is not a war-powers right per se — the document does not specify war and defense as standalone foundational decision rights, in keeping with the scope-out-with-threading standing the sixth-pass architecture establishes — but the right specifies that executive war-making operates as the paradigm case of executive decisions the right's scale-matched feedback architecture must check, with the substantive criteria for that check drawn from the formal-procedural-allocation analytical work (Fisher, Ely, Dudziak), the substantive normative work (Arendt, Walzer engaged critically, the Anabaptist tradition through its analytical work, Gandhi's *ahimsa* tradition), and the Indigenous-architectural-dispossession analytical work (Dunbar-Ortiz, Estes).
