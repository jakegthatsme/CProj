## Thread A — Opening Framing and the Synthesis-Standing Commitment

This document is the Part XIV capstone of the seventh-pass arc and the document at which the project completes the transition the sixth-pass planning document named: from a corpus of related documents to a single synthesized constitutional-theoretical work. The sixth-pass capstone performed an integrative re-reading at planning-and-anticipation standing — it read the forty-two-document arc as a single cultivation-protocol specification and named the corpus-wide end-to-end synthesis as the milestone a seventh pass would perform. The seventh pass has performed two operations the sixth pass did not: it condensed the forty-two-document arc to thirty-five documents through two targeted consolidations, and it now reads the condensed arc end-to-end at synthesis standing. This document is that reading.

The distinction between planning-and-anticipation standing and synthesis standing is not a distinction of length or of register; it is a distinction of what the document does. The v6 capstone read the arc and specified what an end-to-end synthesis would have to accomplish. The v7 capstone accomplishes it: it reads the consolidated corpus as a single artifact, performs the cross-cutting integrative moves the corpus's productive tensions and silent gaps require, draws the diagnostic-to-prescriptive bridge the consolidation makes legible, and states the project's analytical position as settled by the arc's substantive work rather than as named for downstream completion. The settlement the v6 capstone reached on the renovation-versus-decolonial question — that cultivation is legitimate under the multi-traditional canonical commitment and is not synthesis-as-appropriation — is carried forward here not as a position the synthesis will earn but as one the completed arc has earned, performed at the level of the work as a whole.

The load-bearing triad operates as the reading frame for the synthesis and is read once, not re-argued. Biocultural governance is the constitutional ontology: the polity is a complex adaptive system cultivated across nested spatial and temporal scales as a biocultural formation, with biological substrate and cultural plurality coupled, grounded in the biocultural-diversity and biocultural-rights literatures and articulated by the Indigenous and Eastern traditions as primary articulators (`III.01_Biocultural_Governance_v7`). Eco-mimetic policy-making is the operational method: ecology, complex-adaptive-systems theory, biomimicry, and disturbance-ecology concepts inspire how laws are passed, written, and tested (`XII.01_Eco_Mimetic_Policy_Making_v7`). Disturbance-adapted implementation is the temporal orientation: intergenerational architecture cultivated to absorb a plural disturbance landscape comprising five named regimes and their interactions (`II.02_Five_Regime_Disturbance_Diagnostic_v7`, `II.08_Disturbance_Regime_Interactions_v7`, and the temporal commitment of `XIII.01_Decision_Rights_Synthesis_v7`). The Western complex-adaptive-systems, ecology, biomimicry, and disturbance-ecology canons are recent partial recovery of what the Indigenous and Eastern traditions have carried for centuries. "Grown, not built" is the colloquial summary phrase. The synthesis demonstrates how each Part of the consolidated arc instantiates one or more of the triad's elements; it does not re-derive the triad.

The corpus the synthesis reads is the condensed thirty-four-document active arc (the thirty-five-document arc inclusive of this capstone). The seventh-pass condensation consolidated the five Part-II disturbance-regime diagnostics into `II.02_Five_Regime_Disturbance_Diagnostic_v7`, with one major section per regime, and the four Part-XII bounded-decision-rights documents into `XII.09_Bounded_Decision_Rights_Across_Nested_Scales_v7`, with one major section per scale band. The consolidation removed the structural-template repetition that the sixth-pass arc carried — five regime diagnostics sharing an identical diagnostic template, four decision-rights documents sharing an identical four-element specification template — while preserving each regime's analytical apparatus and each scale band's decision-rights specification in its own register. The consolidation is not merely a reduction in document count; it is the condition under which the corpus's diagnostic and prescriptive halves each become legible as a single coordinated specification, and the synthesis this capstone performs is in significant part the reading the consolidation makes possible. The capstone's substantive work proceeds across the seven remaining threads: the consolidated-corpus reading of the fourteen Parts (Thread B); the triad instantiation across the arc (Thread C); the regime-to-scale-band mapping the consolidation makes legible (Thread D); the renovation-versus-decolonial settlement at synthesis standing (Thread E); the multi-traditional canonical commitment as constitutional method, with its cross-tradition seams and its realized-depth asymmetries named (Thread F); the four new Part-level documents and the five new threads carried forward at synthesis standing, with the thread asymmetry registered (Thread G); and the closing reading of the synthesized arc as the project's transition from corpus to work, with the deferred work named (Thread H).

## Thread C — Triad Instantiation Across the Consolidated Arc

The synthesis demonstrates how the consolidated arc instantiates the load-bearing triad — the integrative move the per-Part documents cannot carry, because each instantiates the triad locally while the arc instantiates it across the nested scales and the disturbance landscape as a whole.

Biocultural governance as constitutional ontology is instantiated across the whole arc, not only in Part III where it is articulated. The disturbance regimes of `II.02` are diagnosed as disturbances to the *biocultural* substrate — the cultural regime acting on cultural plurality, the biological regime on the biological substrate, the cognitive regime on the cognitive substrate, with the regime distinctions themselves a biocultural analytic. The material-substrate cultivation of Part VI is the cultivation of the biocultural formation's material conditions; the bounded decision rights of `XII.09` are scale-matched to the nested-scales reference frame the biocultural ontology specifies, with the foundational rights at biosphere-and-ecozone scale and the cognitive-and-intergenerational rights at organism-and-organ-system scale because the biocultural formation is constituted across precisely those nested scales. The ontology is not a Part among Parts; it is the frame within which every Part's work is conducted.

Eco-mimetic policy-making as operational method is instantiated wherever the architecture specifies how its commitments are drafted, enacted, monitored, and adapted. `XII.01` articulates the method — the legislative-cycle architecture (the r-phase, K-phase, Ω-phase, and α-phase of the adaptive cycle the panarchy literature `V.01` carries specifies), the legal-drafting-as-pattern-language register, and the ecological-monitoring-style constitutional-and-statutory review — and the consolidated `XII.09` operationalizes it: the four-element specification format is the eco-mimetic legal-architectural pattern applied at fourteen decision rights, with the feedback condition reading judicial review and public check as feedback architecture in the eco-mimetic sense rather than as terminal adjudication, and the sunset-or-adaptation condition matching the review cycle to the scale-band dynamics the right governs. The method is not a Part among Parts; it is the operational grammar in which the structural commitments are specified.

Disturbance-adapted implementation as temporal orientation is instantiated across the diagnostic-to-prescriptive arc. Part II diagnoses the compound-disturbance landscape as the standing condition under which the architecture operates — not as exceptional disruption to a presumed steady state — and `XII.09` absorbs it: the bounded decision rights are specified with sunset-or-adaptation conditions precisely because the architecture must remain revisable under a disturbance landscape that does not hold still, and the intergenerational criterion of `XII.09`'s organism-and-temporal scale band (the Haudenosaunee seventh-generation criterion at primary articulation, with Brown Weiss's three-obligation specification and the Welsh Future Generations operational practice as comparative recovery) carries the temporal orientation as a decision-rule input across every band. The orientation is not a Part among Parts; it is the temporal frame within which the diagnostic landscape and the prescriptive architecture are coupled.

The three triad elements are not three separable commitments the arc happens to carry; they are three readings of a single commitment — that the polity is a biocultural formation to be cultivated, by an eco-mimetic method, under a disturbance-adapted temporal orientation — and the consolidated arc instantiates the single commitment across its fourteen Parts. The consolidation sharpens the instantiation: with the five regimes in one document and the four scale bands in one document, the triad's reading frame applies to two coordinated specifications rather than to nine separate ones, and the arc's character as a single cultivation-protocol specification rather than a corpus of related documents is more legible at the consolidated structure than it was at the sixth-pass structure.

## Thread D — The Regime-to-Scale-Band Mapping: The Synthesis the Consolidation Makes Legible

The seventh-pass consolidation places the project's diagnostic and prescriptive halves into two coordinated documents — the five-regime diagnostic `II.02` and the four-scale-band bounded-decision-rights specification `XII.09` — and in doing so makes drawable a mapping no document in the corpus draws: which of the five disturbance regimes are absorbed by which of the four scale-band decision rights, and through which of `II.08`'s cross-regime feedback conditions the absorption is coordinated. The mapping is the diagnostic-to-prescriptive bridge at the consolidated structure. It is a synthesis move and not an enumeration, because its analytical content is that the two axes — regime (domain) and scale band (the nested-scales reference frame) — are orthogonal, and the architecture's organization of the decision rights by scale rather than by regime is itself the answer to the cross-scale amplification `II.08` diagnoses.

The orthogonality is the point. The disturbance landscape is diagnosed by regime because each regime acts on a distinct substrate through a distinct analytical apparatus; the decision rights are specified by scale band because the architecture must absorb disturbance at the scale where its compound effect lands, which `II.08`'s non-additivity finding (the compound effect of disturbances in multiple regimes exceeds the sum of the regime-by-regime effects) shows is not the scale of any single regime's origin. A regime-organized architecture — one decision-rights document per disturbance regime — would systematically misfit the compound-disturbance landscape, because the compound effect is cross-regime and cross-scale. A scale-organized architecture absorbs the compound effect at the scale band where it lands, with each band's decision rights answering the disturbances of multiple regimes simultaneously. The mapping is therefore many-to-many: each regime's disturbances are absorbed across several scale bands, and each scale band's rights absorb several regimes' disturbances.

The political-economic regime — currency-and-monetary shocks, supply-chain collapse, debt-crisis cascades, technological labor displacement, capital concentration — is absorbed most directly by the foundational band's unidirectional dual currency convertibility, which constrains the capacity of political-economic decisions to dissolve ecological standing through monetary substitution at biospheric scale, and by the public-check band's public-trust threshold for private organizations, which checks capital concentration where private-organization operation crosses into public-trust terrain. It is further absorbed by the provisioning band's publicly-funded-research-returns right (which answers the capital appropriation of public research outputs) and the material-substrate rights (which answer the commodification of land, energy, food, and care). The capture architecture `II.03` diagnoses is the concrete bridge through which the political-economic regime couples to the cultural regime, and the multi-lens checks-and-balances of the cognitive-and-intergenerational band carries the political lens through which the coupling is checked.

The cognitive-and-informational regime — disinformation cascades, attention-capture architectures, AI-driven epistemic disruption, cognitive-load shocks, generational cognitive-substrate shifts — is absorbed most directly by the public-check band's technological-critique response, which provides the scale-matched public check on AI, surveillance technology, and the broader technological-development cycle before deployment forecloses deliberation, and the e-democracy immediate-public-check, which answers the erosion of the cognitive commons through the deliberative substrate. It is further absorbed by the cognitive-and-intergenerational band's neurodivergent-polis cultivation conditions, which secure cognitive plurality at organism scale against the regime's pressure toward a single cognitive baseline, and by the multi-lens checks-and-balances' epistemic lens (the multi-eyed-seeing `VII.01` carries). The cultural-cognitive coupling `II.08` specifies — media capture reproducing oligarchy, disinformation reproducing racism, attention-architecture shifts reproducing caste — means the cognitive regime's absorption cannot be separated from the cultural regime's, and the architecture's response operates across both bands simultaneously.

The cultural regime — the five axes of caste, oligarchy, media, racism, and embodiment, with the sexuality-and-gender-and-reproduction and arts-and-language-and-cultural-production threads integrated at the embodiment and media axes — is absorbed across the most bands, because the cultural substrate is the substrate through which the polity's plurality is reproduced or captured at every scale. The embodiment axis is absorbed by the cognitive-and-intergenerational band's gender-and-sexuality organism-scale rights and neurodivergent-polis conditions (the neuroqueer coupling), by the public-check band's scale-matched check on administrative, legislative, and institutional decisions bearing on bodily, reproductive, and gender autonomy (administrative violence as the paradigm terrain), and by the foundational band's bodily and reproductive autonomy as foundational decision rights. The oligarchy axis couples to the political-economic regime's absorption (the currency convertibility and the public-trust threshold). The media axis couples to the cognitive regime's absorption (the technological-critique response and e-democracy). The racism and caste axes are checked by the multi-lens architecture's justice lens (the multi-traditional articulation `I.01` carries) and the harm-response structural commitment `XII.07` carries. The cultural regime's absorption is the clearest case of the many-to-many mapping: a single regime's five axes are absorbed across all four scale bands.

The biological regime — pandemic dynamics, biodiversity loss, food-web and soil-system breakdown, vector-borne disease shift, antibiotic resistance — is absorbed most directly by the provisioning band's material-substrate rights (the right to health provisioning from `VI.06`, the food-soil-agriculture provisioning from `VI.02`, the land-and-resource sovereignty and biodiversity stewardship from `VI.01`) and by the foundational band's currency convertibility (which constrains the biospheric-impact political-economic decisions that drive biological disturbance). The biological-political-economic coupling `II.08` specifies — the pandemic-and-supply-chain coupling, the pharmaceutical political economy — couples the biological regime's absorption to the foundational currency convertibility and the public-check public-trust threshold.

The physical-environmental regime — climate, hydrological regime shift, fire regime shift, geological events, anthropogenic chemical contamination — is absorbed most directly by the foundational band's unidirectional dual currency convertibility, which is the architecture's single most direct biospheric-scale absorption of physical-environmental disturbance (the unidirectional constraint protects ecological standing against monetary substitution), and by the foundational band's judicial review of executive orders (the scale-matched feedback on climate-policy and trade-policy executive orders with biospheric reach). It is further absorbed by the provisioning band's material-substrate rights (energy from `VI.03`, land and water from `VI.01` and `VI.02`, the built environment from `VI.05`). Climate, as `IX.01` specifies, is the paradigmatic stress test through which the whole architecture's adequacy is assessed; the physical-environmental regime's absorption across the foundational and provisioning bands is the architecture's answer to that stress test.

`II.08`'s six cross-regime feedback conditions are the operational bridge through which the many-to-many mapping is coordinated. Because each regime's disturbances couple to the others — the cultural-cognitive, cultural-political-economic, cultural-physical-environmental, biological-political-economic, biological-physical-environmental, and cognitive-political-economic couplings `II.08` specifies at depth — the scale-band rights' feedback-condition element (the eco-mimetic monitoring `XII.01` operationalizes, with diverse-epistemic-standing monitoring at parity) must enable cross-regime feedback rather than regime-by-regime feedback. The architecture's recovery-interference problem (recovery from one disturbance is impaired by the presence of others) is answered by the cross-scale coupling the consolidated `XII.09` specifies: the foundational rights condition the operational capacity of the finer-scale rights precisely so that the architecture does not absorb each regime's disturbance in isolation. The regime-to-scale-band mapping, drawn at the consolidated structure, is the synthesis the per-document corpus could not perform: it shows that the bounded-decision-rights architecture is organized by scale rather than by regime because the compound-disturbance landscape is a single cross-scale landscape, and that the architecture's adequacy to the landscape turns on the cross-regime feedback the four-element format's feedback condition enables.

## Thread E — The Renovation-Versus-Decolonial Settlement at Synthesis Standing

The renovation-versus-decolonial question runs through the project from the fifth-pass framing forward, and the v6 capstone reached a settled position on it at planning-and-anticipation standing. The v7 capstone performs the settlement at synthesis standing — not as a position the synthesis will earn, but as one the completed arc has earned, read at the level of the work as a whole.

The decolonial critique, in its strongest articulation, holds that any single-document constitutional synthesis drawing on multiple traditions performs an act of appropriation by virtue of the synthesis-into-architecture, regardless of how carefully the source traditions are preserved. The critique is engaged directly across the arc — Walter Mignolo's coloniality framework, Aníbal Quijano's coloniality-of-power matrix, María Lugones's coloniality of gender, Boaventura de Sousa Santos's epistemicide, Eve Tuck (Unangax̂) and K. Wayne Yang's decolonization-is-not-a-metaphor, Linda Martín Alcoff's analytical politics of whiteness, and the broader Modernity/Coloniality corpus — in `VII.01`, `III.01`, and `XIII.01`, where the project does not hold the critique at arm's length but operates it as a live methodological discipline within its own work.

The project's settled analytical position, earned by the arc's substantive work and not declared, is that cultivation is legitimate under the multi-traditional canonical commitment and is not synthesis-as-appropriation. The legitimacy is operationalized through three disciplines the synthesis performs rather than asserts. Cultivation specifies a discipline, not a fixed architecture: the cultivation protocol `XIII.01` specifies is not a constitutional specification that fixes the relations between the traditions at founding but a discipline under which each tradition continues to articulate constitutional theory in its own analytical apparatus through the architecture's ongoing constitutional method — and the consolidation makes this more visible, not less, because the consolidated `II.02` and `XII.09` preserve each regime's and each scale band's analytical apparatus in its own register within the single document rather than synthesizing them into a single vocabulary. Cultivation operates through cross-citation rather than synthesis-into-single-vocabulary: this capstone itself is the paradigmatic case, integrating the corpus through cross-citation under the move-don't-regenerate discipline, with each tradition's analytical apparatus operating in its own register through the upstream documents and the integration operating at the level of architectural coordination rather than analytical synthesis. Cultivation operates as ongoing rather than as fixed-at-founding: the architecture maintains the conditions across time, with the traditions whose articulations it coordinates entitled to participate in the ongoing methodological work as primary articulators rather than as subjects of synthesis the project performs on them, and the sunset-or-adaptation condition of every bounded decision right in `XII.09` is the architectural form of that ongoing revisability.

The settlement is not foreclosure of the critique, and the synthesis performs the tension rather than resolving it. The cultivation framing's response is itself subject to the critique's ongoing pressure; the protocol does not claim to have resolved the critique but to have advanced a constitutional proposal under the cultivation framing that distinguishes itself substantively from the design-renovation framing the critique most sharply identifies, while remaining subject to the critique's ongoing discipline. The decolonial critique remains a live methodological discipline within the project. This is the corpus's deepest productive tension — a constitutional synthesis that holds open the critique of constitutional synthesis — and the synthesis-standing performance of it is not to dissolve the tension into a resolution but to demonstrate that the completed arc has held it: the project has produced an integrated constitutional architecture while keeping the critique of integration operative within the architecture's own method. The cultivation discipline includes the capacity for revision from sources the project does not author — the traditions whose primary articulators the project engages, the communities and Indigenous nations the protocol supports, and the cultivation work itself across the intergenerational horizon. The position is settled in the sense that the arc's work has earned it; it is not settled in the sense of closing the disciplines under which the project operates.

## Thread F — The Multi-Traditional Canonical Commitment at Synthesis Standing

The multi-traditional canonical commitment runs through the project as constitutional method, not as comparative supplement or token engagement. The Indigenous and Eastern traditions are primary articulators of all three triad elements; the Western complex-adaptive-systems, ecology, biomimicry, and disturbance-ecology canons are recent partial recovery. The arc engages the Indigenous constitutional traditions (the Haudenosaunee Kaianerekowa and the seventh-generation criterion; the Andean *sumak kawsay* and the Pachamama-as-constitutional-subject commitment incorporated in the Ecuadorian and Bolivian constitutions; the Maori co-governance commitment of *Matike Mai Aotearoa*; the Aboriginal Australian Uluru Statement; the Diné *Hózhó* and *Diné Bi Beenahaz'áanii*; the Pacific Islander *Vā* and 'Epeli Hau'ofa's "Our Sea of Islands"; the Sámi *siida*), the Eastern traditions (the Daoist *wu wei* and *ziran*; the Buddhist dependent-origination; the Confucian remonstrance and *li*; the Islamic *shura* and *waqf*), the African traditions (the Kurukan Fuga Charter; the *ujamaa* tradition; the African-feminist canon of Amadiume and Oyěwùmí), the Black-radical and Caribbean canons (Cedric Robinson's racial-capitalism; Du Bois; the Combahee River Collective; Césaire, Fanon, Wynter, Glissant), the dalit tradition (Ambedkar; Teltumbde; Yengde), the Latin American Liberation-Theology and Liberation-pedagogy traditions, the disability-justice and neurodivergent canons, the feminist care-ethics and social-reproduction canons, the Latinx and Asian-American canons, the climate-justice canon, and the Russian/Eastern-European and Jewish critical canons — each engaged in its own analytical register through the upstream documents, under the multi-eyed-seeing methodological discipline `VII.01` carries.

At synthesis standing, two facts about the multi-traditional commitment that the per-document work could leave implicit must be named, because the synthesis is the place where the corpus's treatment of its own canon is read as a whole.

The first is that the multi-traditional canon is not a unified block, and the synthesis must name the cross-tradition seams rather than gesturing at a single co-equal plurality. Several traditions the arc holds at co-equal standing make claims in genuine tension with one another, and the cultivation discipline's commitment is to hold the tensions in their own registers rather than to dissolve them. The dalit anti-caste tradition (Ambedkar's *Annihilation of Caste*) stands in tension with the inheritances some other traditions carry, and the arc's engagement of caste as an analytical axis of the cultural-disturbance regime in `II.02` preserves the dalit specification in its own register rather than absorbing it into a race-frame or a class-frame. The Black-radical tradition's racial-capitalism analysis (Cedric Robinson) and the Indigenous land-sovereignty-primacy analysis (the Land Back articulation `VI.01` carries, Coulthard's *Red Skin, White Masks*) operate at a genuine seam that Tiffany Lethabo King's *The Black Shoals: Offshore Formations of Black and Native Studies* works directly — the seam between a tradition centered on the afterlife of slavery and racial capital and a tradition centered on settler-colonial dispossession of land — and the arc engages both at primary-articulator standing without subordinating either to the other's frame. The universalist register of some rights-traditions stands in tension with the relational-ontology register of others, and the bounded-decision-rights architecture of `XII.09` holds both: the rights are specified in the four-element format the rights-tradition recognizes, while the feedback condition and the diverse-epistemic-standing monitoring carry the relational-ontology commitment that decisions are made in reciprocity with human and broader-than-human members. The synthesis names these seams because the cultivation discipline's legitimacy turns on holding them open rather than resolving them into a single constitutional vocabulary.

The second is that the arc's realized engagement of the Eastern traditions is asymmetric with its stated co-equal standing, and the synthesis must name the asymmetry honestly rather than present an even co-equal engagement the corpus does not in fact realize. The Indigenous traditions carry the substantive analytical load across the arc — the regime diagnostics, the material-substrate cultivation, the bounded decision rights, and the methodological discipline all lean on Indigenous primary articulators engaged at analytical depth throughout. The Eastern traditions are engaged at genuine analytical depth in a smaller set of documents: the cognitive-and-informational regime section of `II.02` (the Buddhist *bhavanga* and Abhidhamma consciousness analysis, the Madhyamaka two-truths analysis through Nāgārjuna and Jay Garfield, the Daoist contemplative traditions); `III.01`'s engagement of the dependent-origination tradition; `II.08`'s Thread C (Macy's *Mutual Causality* as the bridge between dependent-origination and the systems-theoretic apparatus); and `XII.01`'s engagement of the Daoist, Buddhist, Confucian, and Islamic method articulators. In other documents the Eastern traditions appear as named-articulator lists rather than as engaged analytical apparatus. The asymmetry is a thin-versus-rich asymmetry between the stated co-equal Eastern-and-Indigenous standing and the realized depth, and the synthesis names it as such: the commitment to Eastern traditions at co-equal primary-articulator standing is genuine and is realized at depth where the analytical terrain calls for it, but the arc's center of analytical gravity is Indigenous, and the project's continued work under the cultivation discipline includes the deepening of the Eastern engagement at the sites where it currently rests at the list level. Naming the asymmetry is itself an operation of the cultivation discipline: the discipline includes the capacity for revision from sources the project does not author, and the Eastern traditions' primary articulators are entitled to deepen the arc's engagement of their contributions as the cultivation work proceeds.

The multi-traditional canonical commitment, read at synthesis standing, is constitutional method realized unevenly but genuinely across the arc, with the cross-tradition seams held open and the realized-depth asymmetries named. The substantive form of the commitment is the engagement of each tradition's primary articulators in that tradition's own analytical apparatus through the upstream documents, with the multi-eyed-seeing discipline as the coordinating method and the cultivation discipline as the ongoing constitutional method through which the engagement — including its deepening at the thin sites — proceeds across time.

## Thread H — Closing Synthesis: The Work, Not the Corpus; the Next 250 Years; the Deferred Work

The synthesized arc reads as the project's transition from a corpus of related documents to a single constitutional-theoretical work. The sixth pass produced the substantive analytical commitments across forty-two documents and read them at planning-and-anticipation standing; the seventh pass condensed the corpus to thirty-five documents and reads it at synthesis standing. At synthesis standing the arc is a work and not a corpus: the five-regime diagnostic and the four-scale-band architecture read as two coordinated specifications rather than nine separate documents, the diagnostic-to-prescriptive bridge is drawn at the regime-to-scale-band mapping, the productive tensions are performed rather than named for downstream completion, and the project's analytical position is stated as settled by the arc's substantive work. The transition from corpus to work is what the seventh pass accomplishes, and this capstone is the document at which the transition is performed.

What the synthesis accomplishes is the analytical case — the case for biocultural governance as constitutional ontology, eco-mimetic policy-making as operational method, disturbance-adapted implementation as temporal orientation, and the cultivation protocol of `XIII.01` as the integrated constitutional architecture under those commitments, read across the consolidated arc at synthesis standing. What the synthesis does not accomplish is the cultivation work itself, which is the intergenerational work the protocol is offered to support. The architecture is not built when the protocol is synthesized; the architecture grows through the cultivation work the protocol's ongoing constitutional method conducts across the nested-scales reference frame, across the disturbance landscape the architecture inhabits, across the intergenerational horizon the temporal commitment specifies, and across the multi-traditional canonical plurality the methodological commitment honors. The synthesis is the project's analytical contribution at the seventh pass; the cultivation work is the next 250 years and beyond.

The synthesis names the work it defers. The defense-and-war-powers question is carried at scope-out-with-threading standing through the consolidated `XII.09`'s foundational and public-check scale bands, and its thinness relative to the other four sixth-pass threads (Thread G) is partly a principled scope decision and partly a flag for future work. The v6 capstone named a Part-level standalone document on defense, war powers, and the constitutional commitments to non-militarism as a substantive addition a future pass could take up; the seventh-pass condensation, undertaken at moderate scope, did not add it, and the v7 capstone carries the flag forward: the defense-and-war-powers standalone treatment is eighth-pass-or-later candidate work, not a gap the seventh pass silently closes or silently drops. The deepening of the Eastern-traditions engagement at the sites where it currently rests at the list level (Thread F) is likewise carried forward as continued work under the cultivation discipline. The eighth pass is not specified in the seventh pass; were it undertaken, it would be specified in its own succession document, and the project's analytical position at the seventh pass is offered as substrate for it rather than as terminus.

The position is settled in the sense that the arc's substantive work has earned it; it is not settled in the sense of foreclosing the disciplines the project operates under. The disciplines are ongoing constitutional method, not closed at synthesis. The cultivation discipline includes the capacity for revision from sources the project does not author — the traditions whose primary articulators the project engages, the communities and Indigenous nations the protocol supports, the cross-tradition seams the synthesis holds open, the realized-depth asymmetries the synthesis names, and the cultivation work itself across the intergenerational horizon. The decolonial critique remains a live methodological discipline within the project; the cultivation framing's response to it is itself subject to the critique's ongoing pressure; and the renovation-versus-decolonial settlement is performed at synthesis standing not as a resolution that closes the question but as a demonstration that the completed arc has held the question open within its own method.

The architecture is not a building. It is a cultivation protocol. The protocol is grown, not built. The growing is the work the architecture's ongoing constitutional method conducts across time, with the multi-traditional traditions, the communities and Indigenous nations the polity comprises, and the cultivation discipline's ongoing constitutional method together conducting it. The protocol becomes the architecture not at founding and not at synthesis but through the cultivation work the protocol is offered to support. The seventh pass closes the corpus-to-work transition; the cultivation work the protocol is offered to support extends across the next 250 years and beyond. The architecture grows.
