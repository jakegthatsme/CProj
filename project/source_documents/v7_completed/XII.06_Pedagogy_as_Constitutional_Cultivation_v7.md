## PART ONE — PEDAGOGY AS CONSTITUTIONAL CULTIVATION ACROSS DEVELOPMENTAL AND INTERGENERATIONAL SCALES

### 1.1 The cultivation framing

The cultivation framing reads pedagogy as constitutional work across two temporal scales of the nested-scales reference frame. At the developmental scale, pedagogy is the cultivation of the growing person across the years of childhood and adolescence within which the cognitive, affective, social, and bodymind architecture of the human person develops. At the intergenerational scale, pedagogy is the cultivation of biocultural transmission: the institutional practices through which one generation transmits, revises, and grows the polity's biocultural inheritance for the generations that follow. The cultivation framing reads both scales as constitutional rather than as policy-domain.

The framing operates against the contemporary configuration's framing of education as service-delivery. Under the service-delivery framing, education is a service the polity provides through public and private institutional architectures, with the service's outputs measured by standardized assessment and the service's function specified by reference to subsequent labor-market and credentialing pathways. The framing locates education within the broader political-economic configuration's commodity-form apparatus, with the structural-political-economic consequence that educational access is determined principally through private-purchasing-power distribution (the contemporary American educational configuration's combination of property-tax-funded public schools producing systematic inter-jurisdictional inequality, private-school configurations operating outside the public configuration, and the tertiary-education tuition-and-debt configuration the contemporary literature has engaged at length).

The cultivation framing operates differently. Pedagogy under the cultivation framing is not service the polity provides but cultivation work the polity conducts on itself: the institutional practices through which the polity transmits, revises, and grows. The structural-political-architectural implication is that pedagogy is constitutional commitment rather than policy-domain provision, that the institutional architecture engages the developmental and intergenerational scales as constitutive of the broader constitutional cultivation work, and that the integration with `XII.04_Intergenerational_Rectification_v7`'s intergenerational rectification commitment is direct.

### 1.2 The internal-coherence argument

The internal-coherence argument articulates that engaging neurodivergence at organism scale (in `XII.05_Neurodivergent_Polis_v7` and in the cognitive-and-intergenerational decision-rights architecture `XII.09_Bounded_Decision_Rights_Across_Nested_Scales_v7` specifies) while leaving educational architecture unaddressed would be internally incoherent. The cognitive-plurality commitment at organism-and-organ-system scale requires educational architecture that engages cognitive plurality at the developmental scale; the educational architecture is the institutional locus at which cognitive-plurality cultivation is operationalized across the broadest population. The internal-coherence argument therefore specifies that pedagogy is constitutional commitment rather than policy-domain provision, with the educational-architectural reconstruction operating jointly with the cognitive-plurality and intergenerational-rectification commitments.

### 1.3 The triad's bearing on pedagogy

The biocultural-governance ontology specifies that the polity is a complex adaptive system cultivated across nested spatial and temporal scales as a biocultural formation, with biological substrate and cultural plurality coupled. Pedagogy is the principal institutional practice through which the biocultural formation is sustained, with the institutional architecture engaging both the biological-developmental substrate (the cognitive-bodymind development of the growing person) and the cultural-transmission substrate (the institutional practices through which cultural plurality is transmitted across generations). The eco-mimetic policy-making method specifies that the pedagogical architecture is grown rather than built — cultivated as adaptive system rather than engineered as standardized-content-delivery apparatus. The disturbance-adapted implementation specifies that the pedagogical architecture is cultivated to absorb the plural disturbance landscape across the cultural, biological, physical-environmental, cognitive-and-informational, and political-economic disturbance regimes.

## PART TWO — THE STANDARDIZATION-AND-TESTING DIAGNOSTIC

### 2.1 The contemporary configuration

The contemporary American educational configuration operates principally through standardization-and-testing apparatus institutionalized across multiple decades. The configuration's foundational legislative architecture includes the Elementary and Secondary Education Act of 1965 (and its subsequent reauthorizations including the No Child Left Behind Act of 2001 and the Every Student Succeeds Act of 2015), the Individuals with Disabilities Education Act (originally the Education for All Handicapped Children Act of 1975), and the broader federal-and-state educational-regulatory apparatus. The configuration's contemporary operational content includes standardized testing as the principal measure of student learning and institutional performance, accountability systems that operate through standardized-testing outputs, content-standards apparatus that specifies what students should know at specified grade levels, and the broader content-delivery configuration through which the contemporary educational apparatus operates.

### 2.2 Jonathan Kozol on structural inequality and resegregation

Jonathan Kozol's *Savage Inequalities: Children in America's Schools* (Crown, 1991) supplies the foundational contemporary documentary record on the structural inequality of American schooling. Kozol's documentary contribution operates through the systematic engagement with American public schools across multiple jurisdictions, with the empirical record demonstrating that the property-tax-funded public-school configuration produces systematic inter-jurisdictional inequality whose content places students in low-tax-base communities at substantial educational disadvantage relative to students in high-tax-base communities. The empirical record across the schools Kozol engaged — the East St. Louis schools, the schools of the South Side of Chicago, the schools of Camden, New Jersey, and the broader documentary record — articulates the empirical foundation for the structural-architectural critique of the contemporary configuration.

Kozol's *The Shame of the Nation: The Restoration of Apartheid Schooling in America* (Crown, 2005) extends the documentary record through the engagement with the post-*Brown v. Board of Education* resegregation of American schools across multiple decades. The empirical record articulates that the contemporary American educational configuration has produced systematic resegregation across multiple dimensions (racial resegregation in the schools of major American metropolitan areas, structural-political-economic resegregation through the public-private school configuration, the broader segregation across charter-school and selective-admission configurations), with the empirical foundation supporting the structural-architectural critique.

### 2.3 Diane Ravitch on the testing-and-accountability regime

Diane Ravitch's *The Death and Life of the Great American School System: How Testing and Choice Are Undermining Education* (Basic Books, 2010) supplies the contemporary analytical apparatus on the testing-and-accountability regime and on the author's documented reversal from advocate to critic of that regime. Ravitch's analytical contribution is consequential precisely because of the reversal: as Assistant Secretary of Education under George H. W. Bush and as a leading advocate of the testing-and-accountability framework across the 1990s and early 2000s, Ravitch was institutionally positioned within the framework she now criticizes. The book articulates the empirical record on the consequences of the testing-and-accountability regime — the narrowing of curriculum to tested content, the displacement of arts, music, civics, and the broader content of liberal education, the production of test-preparation infrastructure that operates against substantive learning, the structural-political-economic alignment of the regime with the broader privatization apparatus the contemporary configuration has institutionalized — and articulates the structural-political-philosophical case for the cultivation framing the present document develops.

### 2.4 What the standardization framing does

The standardization-and-testing framing operates as design-imposition framing: the cognitive-bodymind architecture of the developing person is treated as content-delivery target whose outputs are measured by standardized assessment, with the structural-political-architectural consequence that the developing person is shaped by the assessment apparatus rather than the assessment apparatus shaped by the developing person. The framing produces several structural consequences: the narrowing of pedagogical practice to tested content, the production of high-stakes-testing anxiety that operates as systematic stressor on the developing person, the structural-political-economic alignment of the framing with the contemporary credentialing-and-sorting configuration, the systematic disadvantage of students whose cognitive-bodymind architecture operates outside the narrow band the testing apparatus favors (engaging the cognitive-plurality concerns `XII.05_Neurodivergent_Polis_v7` develops), and the broader structural-political-cultural consequences the contemporary critical-educational scholarship has engaged.

### 2.5 What survives and what does not under the cultivation framing

Under the cultivation framing, the genuine goods of literacy, numeracy, scientific literacy, civic literacy, and shared cultural-historical knowledge survive: the cultivation work engages these as content of the developmental-scale cultivation work. What does not survive is the sorting-and-credentialing function as primary purpose of educational architecture, the testing regime as the principal measure of learning, the standardization of the developing person to a narrow cognitive-functional band, and the structural-political-economic alignment of the educational configuration with the broader commodity-form apparatus the contemporary configuration has institutionalized. The cultivation framing reads literacy, numeracy, and shared knowledge not as products to be delivered but as cultivated capacities that emerge through pedagogical practice oriented to the developing person's actual cognitive-bodymind reality.

## PART THREE — CRITICAL PEDAGOGY

### 3.1 Paulo Freire's *Pedagogy of the Oppressed*

Paulo Freire's *Pedagogy of the Oppressed* (Continuum, 1970; original Portuguese 1968) supplies the foundational contemporary articulation of critical pedagogy. Freire's analytical contribution operates through three foundational concepts. The first is the critique of the banking model of education — the framing of education as deposit of content into the empty container of the learner, with the learner constructed as passive recipient and the educator constructed as knowing depositor. The second is the articulation of conscientization (*conscientização*) as the cultivation of critical awareness of structural-political-cultural conditions through pedagogical practice. The third is the articulation of education as the practice of freedom through dialogue — the cultivation of pedagogical relationship in which educator and learner are co-creators of knowledge, with the dialogue oriented to the cultivation of conscientization and to substantive practice in the world. Freire's *Pedagogy of Hope: Reliving Pedagogy of the Oppressed* (Continuum, 1992; original Portuguese 1992) revisits the foundational work through the contemporary engagement with the political-historical conditions across the decades following the original work, with the analytical contribution articulating the cultivation of pedagogical hope under conditions of structural-political-cultural setback.

The integration with the present argument is direct. The banking-model critique articulates the foundational analytical case against the standardization framing the previous part diagnosed: the banking model is the deeper architectural form of which standardization-and-testing is one institutional manifestation. The articulation of pedagogy as the practice of freedom through dialogue articulates the cultivation framing the present document develops at the level of pedagogical practice. The cultivation of conscientization articulates the structural-political-cultural commitments the pedagogical architecture engages.

### 3.2 bell hooks's engaged pedagogy

bell hooks's *Teaching to Transgress: Education as the Practice of Freedom* (Routledge, 1994) supplies the contemporary American articulation of engaged pedagogy. hooks's analytical contribution operates through the engagement with Freire's apparatus and the extension into the American context. The contribution articulates the cultivation of engaged pedagogy as practice that operates outside the banking-model framing, with the classroom as a location of possibility for the cultivation of critical consciousness, racial-political analysis, gender-political analysis, and the broader content of the political-cultural cultivation work pedagogy can support. hooks's *Teaching Community: A Pedagogy of Hope* (Routledge, 2003) extends the apparatus through engagement with pedagogical practice oriented to community-cultivation, with the analytical contribution articulating the cultivation of pedagogical community as constitutive of educational practice.

The integration with the present argument is direct. hooks's apparatus articulates the cultivation framing at the level of pedagogical practice within the American educational configuration, with the structural-political-cultural commitments engaging the cultural-and-political-economic dimensions of pedagogical work. The integration with `XII.04_Intergenerational_Rectification_v7`'s rectification commitments operates through the engagement of pedagogical work with the structural-historical conditions that produced the contemporary configuration.

### 3.3 What critical pedagogy establishes for the cultivation framing

The critical-pedagogy tradition establishes three commitments for the cultivation framing. The first is that education is never neutral: the pedagogical practice that operates within the contemporary configuration carries the configuration's structural-political-cultural commitments whether or not the practitioners engage that fact, and the cultivation framing requires explicit engagement with the structural-political-cultural content of pedagogical practice. The second is that the banking model is the deeper architectural form of the standardization framing the present document diagnoses: the banking model and the standardization framing are continuous rather than separable, with the analytical case against one operating as analytical case against the other. The third is that the cultivation framing is the cultivation of pedagogical dialogue and of the learner as co-creator of knowledge: the structural-architectural alternative to the banking model is not a different mode of delivery but a different relational practice in which educator and learner together cultivate the content of the learning work.

## PART FOUR — INDIGENOUS PEDAGOGY

### 4.1 Linda Tuhiwai Smith on decolonizing methodologies

Linda Tuhiwai Smith (Ngāti Awa, Ngāti Porou) and her *Decolonizing Methodologies: Research and Indigenous Peoples* (Zed Books, 1999; second edition 2012) supplies foundational contemporary articulation of the Indigenous-pedagogy tradition's critical work. Smith's analytical contribution operates through the systematic engagement with the historical-structural-political conditions under which Western research and Western education have operated as instruments of colonization across Indigenous peoples globally. The contribution articulates the structural-political case that Western educational institutions have functioned as colonizing institutions across multiple Indigenous contexts, with the implications for contemporary pedagogical-architectural reconstruction operating at the structural-political-architectural level rather than at the level of accommodation within the existing configuration.

Smith's articulation of Kaupapa Māori pedagogy — the cultivation of pedagogical practice grounded in Māori cultural-philosophical commitments, in *tikanga Māori* (Māori law and practice), and in Māori-sovereignty commitments — supplies one of the contemporary articulations of Indigenous pedagogy as complete and prior pedagogical tradition. The Kaupapa Māori apparatus operates not as Māori-cultural variant within a broader Western pedagogical framework but as pedagogical tradition operating on its own constitutive commitments. The integration with the present argument operates at primary-articulator standing.

### 4.2 Marie Battiste on cognitive imperialism and the learning spirit

Marie Battiste (Mi'kmaq) and her *Decolonizing Education: Nourishing the Learning Spirit* (Purich Publishing, 2013) supplies the contemporary articulation of Indigenous pedagogy in the Canadian context. Battiste's analytical contribution operates through the articulation of cognitive imperialism — the structural-political-cultural operation through which Western cognitive-pedagogical frameworks have been imposed on Indigenous peoples through educational institutions — and through the articulation of the nourishment of the learning spirit as cultivation framework distinct from the Western educational apparatus. The contribution articulates that Indigenous pedagogy operates through the cultivation of the learner's relationship to land, to language, to community, to ancestry, and to spirit, with the structural-political-architectural commitments engaging the cultivation of the learning spirit as constitutive of pedagogical work.

The integration with the present argument operates at primary-articulator standing. Battiste's articulation supplies pedagogical-architectural vocabulary the Western pedagogical apparatus does not generate from within itself: the cultivation of the learning spirit, the structural-political-cultural commitments to Indigenous-language pedagogical work, the integration of pedagogical practice with land-based and community-grounded cultivation, and the broader content of the Indigenous-pedagogical apparatus.

### 4.3 Gregory Cajete on Native science and education as journey of becoming

Gregory Cajete (Tewa, Santa Clara Pueblo) and his *Look to the Mountain: An Ecology of Indigenous Education* (Kivaki Press, 1994) and *Native Science: Natural Laws of Interdependence* (Clear Light, 2000) supply the contemporary articulation of Indigenous pedagogy as ecology of education grounded in Indigenous epistemology. Cajete's analytical contribution operates through the articulation of education as journey of becoming related to place and community, with the pedagogical work specified as the cultivation of the learner's substantive relationships to land, to community, to cultural-philosophical inheritance, and to the broader cosmological framework Indigenous traditions cultivate. The articulation of Native science operates as cultivation of Indigenous epistemological apparatus that engages the natural world through specific Indigenous-cosmological commitments rather than through the Western scientific apparatus alone.

The integration with the present argument operates at primary-articulator standing. Cajete's apparatus articulates the pedagogical-cultivation framework that operates on Indigenous-cosmological commitments rather than as Indigenous-cultural variant of the Western pedagogical apparatus.

### 4.4 What the Indigenous-pedagogy tradition articulates

The Indigenous-pedagogy tradition articulates three commitments that the Euro-American traditions do not: that education is the transmission of a people's relationship to land and to one another, that the standardized school is itself a colonizing instrument operating against Indigenous-pedagogical work, and that Indigenous pedagogy is a complete and prior pedagogical tradition rather than a culturally specific variant of a more general pedagogical framework. The implication for the constitutional cultivation work is that Indigenous-pedagogical sovereignty is constitutional commitment: the institutional architecture supports the cultivation of Indigenous-pedagogical work on its own constitutive commitments, with the integration with `XI.01_Three_Operational_Layers_v7`'s Indigenous-governance-as-multi-scale-cultivation-vocabulary architecture and with `XII.04_Intergenerational_Rectification_v7`'s Land Back and broader Indigenous-rectification commitments operating at the institutional-architectural level.

## PART FIVE — THE DEMOCRATIC AND PROGRESSIVE SCHOOLING TRADITION

### 5.1 John Dewey on education as growth

John Dewey's *Democracy and Education* (Macmillan, 1916) supplies the foundational American articulation of education as constitutive of democratic life. Dewey's analytical contribution operates through three central commitments. The first is the articulation of education as growth — the cultivation of the learner's capacity to engage in continued growth through experience, with the structural-political-philosophical implication that education is not preparation for life but life itself. The second is the articulation of the continuity of experience — the principle that educational practice is the cultivation of experiences that build on prior experiences and that prepare the learner for subsequent experiences, with the implication for pedagogical practice that the developmental-scale work operates through continuous cultivation rather than discrete content-delivery. The third is the articulation of the school as a form of community life — the conception of school not as preparation for democratic life but as itself a site of democratic practice. Dewey's *Experience and Education* (Kappa Delta Pi, 1938) revisits the apparatus through engagement with the criticisms the progressive-education movement had encountered across the intervening decades.

The integration with the present argument is direct. Dewey's articulation of education as growth operates as foundational articulation of the cultivation framing the present document develops. The integration with `III.01_Biocultural_Governance_v7`'s biocultural-governance ontology is direct: the cultivation of the developing person operates through the cultivation of the learner's engagement with the broader biocultural formation.

### 5.2 Maria Montessori on the prepared environment

Maria Montessori's *The Absorbent Mind* (Holt, 1967; Italian original 1949) and the broader Montessori corpus across her career supply the contemporary articulation of the prepared-environment pedagogy. Montessori's analytical contribution operates through the empirical-observational engagement with the developmental scale of the growing person (her work across multiple decades observed children across multiple developmental stages and across multiple cultural contexts) and the articulation of the prepared environment as cultivation framework. The prepared-environment apparatus operates through the cultivation of pedagogical environments that engage the developing child's substantive interests, capacities, and developmental work rather than imposing predetermined content-delivery sequences on the child.

The integration with the present argument operates through the prepared-environment vocabulary the Montessori apparatus supplies for the cultivation framing. The cultivation work specifies pedagogical environments that engage the developing person's substantive cognitive-bodymind realities rather than imposing standardized content-delivery on the developing person.

### 5.3 Democratic schools: A. S. Neill, Sudbury Valley, Reggio Emilia

A. S. Neill's *Summerhill: A Radical Approach to Child Rearing* (Hart Publishing, 1960) supplies the foundational articulation of democratic schooling. Neill's contribution operates through the empirical-institutional record of the Summerhill school (founded 1921 in Suffolk, England) and through the analytical case for schools governed by their members and for learning freed from compulsion. The Summerhill apparatus articulates the structural-architectural commitments to children's participation in school governance, to the absence of compulsory class attendance, and to the cultivation of learning oriented to the learner's substantive interests rather than to standardized curriculum.

The Sudbury Valley School (founded 1968 in Framingham, Massachusetts) supplies the contemporary American articulation of democratic schooling. The Sudbury Valley apparatus operates through the structural commitments to age-mixing across the broader student body, to school-meeting governance in which each member (student or staff) has one vote regardless of age, and to the cultivation of student-directed learning across the broader pedagogical work. The contemporary documentation, including Daniel Greenberg's *Free at Last: The Sudbury Valley School* (1987) and the broader Sudbury Valley scholarly literature, supplies the contemporary articulation.

The Reggio Emilia approach, developed across the post-war period in the Italian city of Reggio Emilia under the leadership of Loris Malaguzzi and operationalized through the contemporary Reggio Emilia early-childhood education municipal infrastructure, supplies the contemporary European articulation. The Reggio approach articulates the child as competent protagonist of their own learning, the cultivation of the hundred languages of children (the multiple modalities through which children engage and represent their understanding), the role of the environment as third teacher (alongside the human teacher and the parent), and the broader pedagogical-architectural commitments the contemporary Reggio scholarship has cultivated. Carlina Rinaldi's *In Dialogue with Reggio Emilia* (Routledge, 2006) and the broader contemporary Reggio scholarly literature articulate the apparatus.

### 5.4 John Holt and the unschooling literature

John Holt's *How Children Learn* (Pitman, 1967) and the subsequent unschooling literature supply the contemporary articulation of learning that operates outside institutional schooling. Holt's contribution operates through the empirical-observational engagement with how children learn outside instructional configurations and through the structural-political case that the institutional-schooling apparatus systematically interferes with the cultivation of substantive learning. The unschooling literature has cultivated the contemporary articulation of home-based and community-based learning configurations that operate outside the institutional-schooling apparatus.

### 5.5 What the democratic and progressive tradition contributes

The democratic and progressive schooling tradition contributes three commitments to the cultivation framing. The first is the articulation of pedagogical environment as cultivated rather than designed — the school as a cultivated environment that engages the developing person's substantive cognitive-bodymind realities rather than as a delivery system. The second is the articulation of democratic practice as constitutive of pedagogical practice — the cultivation of governance arrangements within schools that engage children as substantive participants. The third is the articulation that learning operates through the developing person's engagement with the broader world rather than through the imposition of standardized content. The thread engages the tradition critically where the progressive tradition has carried class-bound or individualist limitations: the tradition has at times operated through the cultivation of pedagogical practice oriented to specific cultural-and-class formations rather than as broader cultivation work, and the integration with the culturally relevant and culturally sustaining pedagogy tradition Part Seven engages addresses these limitations.

## PART SIX — POPULAR EDUCATION

### 6.1 The Highlander Folk School and Myles Horton

The Highlander Folk School, founded by Myles Horton, Don West, and others in 1932 in Monteagle, Tennessee, and operating in its contemporary form as the Highlander Research and Education Center, supplies the foundational American articulation of popular education. The Highlander apparatus operates as institutional architecture for the cultivation of the capacity of ordinary people to analyze and change their own conditions, with the historical record across the labor-movement period of the 1930s and 1940s and the civil-rights-movement period of the 1950s and 1960s articulating the operational record.

Myles Horton's articulation of popular education, drawing on the influence of the Danish folk-school tradition (Grundtvig and the Danish folk-school movement of the nineteenth century) and on his engagement with the substantive labor and civil-rights organizing work the Highlander institutional architecture supported, articulates the cultivation framework. Horton's *The Long Haul: An Autobiography* (Doubleday, 1990) and his *We Make the Road by Walking: Conversations on Education and Social Change* (with Paulo Freire, Temple University Press, 1990) supply the contemporary articulation. The conversation with Freire supplies the integration with the critical-pedagogy tradition Part Three engaged and articulates the cross-civilizational integration of popular-education and critical-pedagogy commitments.

### 6.2 Highlander in the labor and civil-rights movements

Highlander's institutional contribution to the labor and civil-rights movements operates through the educational architecture the school cultivated. In the labor-movement period, Highlander operated as residential educational center for organizers across the southern labor movement, with the educational practice oriented to the cultivation of organizer capacity through engagement with the conditions the organizers were addressing in their own communities. In the civil-rights-movement period, Highlander operated as residential educational center for civil-rights organizers including Rosa Parks (who attended a Highlander workshop in the summer of 1955, before the Montgomery bus boycott of December 1955), Septima Clark (who developed the citizenship-school methodology at Highlander), Bernice Robinson, Esau Jenkins, and the broader cohort of organizers Highlander cultivated. Septima Clark's citizenship-school methodology — adult literacy education integrated with voter-registration preparation and the cultivation of political-cultural engagement — supplies the contemporary articulation of popular-education methodology adapted to the structural-political-cultural conditions of the Jim Crow South.

### 6.3 What popular education adds

Popular education adds three commitments to the cultivation framing the schooling-centered traditions do not articulate. The first is that pedagogical cultivation is lifelong: the cultivation work does not terminate at the conclusion of formal schooling but continues across the adult life-course as cultivation of the capacities of ordinary people to engage structural-political-cultural conditions. The second is that pedagogical cultivation is not confined to children or to schools: the cultivation work operates across multiple institutional contexts including workplace, community, religious, civic, and the broader institutional architectures within which adults conduct their lives. The third is that pedagogical cultivation is bound up with the cultivation of a polity's capacity for collective self-governance: the cultivation work supports the political-cultural capacities the deliberative-democratic and civic-participation architectures `XII.03_E_Democracy_Structural_Public_Check_v7` engages.

### 7.2 Gloria Ladson-Billings on culturally relevant pedagogy

Gloria Ladson-Billings's *The Dreamkeepers: Successful Teachers of African American Children* (Jossey-Bass, 1994) and her broader scholarly corpus including "Toward a Theory of Culturally Relevant Pedagogy" (*American Educational Research Journal*, 1995) supply the foundational contemporary articulation of culturally relevant pedagogy. The integration with the present argument operates through the articulation that pedagogy either sustains or erodes the cultural plurality the biocultural formation depends on, and that the cultivation framing requires explicit engagement with the cultural-cognitive content of pedagogical practice rather than presupposing a neutral pedagogical configuration.

### 7.3 Django Paris and H. Samy Alim on culturally sustaining pedagogy

Django Paris and H.
