## Unidirectional Dual Currency Convertibility as Foundational Decision Right

The unidirectional dual currency convertibility — operating between a political-economic currency and an ecological currency — is specified as a foundational decision right with the four-element format. Document XII.02 (Currency Architecture) articulates the dual-currency apparatus itself at operational depth: the political-economic currency operating in the conventional money-and-credit register; the ecological currency operating as denomination of ecological standing under the biocultural-governance ontology; the convertibility relation between them, with the unidirectional commitment specifying that conversion proceeds from political-economic into ecological denomination without reverse conversion at any scale at which biospheric impact is at stake. This section specifies the formal bounded-decision-right architecture that conditions the dual-currency apparatus as foundational right rather than as engineerable institutional choice.

**Scope.** The right operates over the convertibility relation between the political-economic currency and the ecological currency. The convertibility relation is the load-bearing architectural commitment in the dual-currency apparatus; the unidirectional specification governs which direction the convertibility runs and under what conditions. The scope is not the dual-currency apparatus itself (Document XII.02's terrain) but the meta-commitment governing the apparatus's operational substrate — the commitment that political-economic decisions cannot dissolve ecological standing through monetary substitution at any scale at which biospheric impact is at stake. The scope is bounded: the right does not specify the substantive content of the ecological-currency denomination (Document XII.02's terrain), nor the cross-scale ecological-currency dynamics (Documents V.01 and X.01's terrain), nor the operational integration with the political-economy architecture (Document X.01 specifies). The right specifies only the convertibility-relation meta-commitment.

**Scale.** Biosphere-and-ecozone scale. The right operates at the scale at which political-economic decisions have aggregate-biospheric reach: monetary architectures whose contraction or expansion bears on aggregate carbon-cycle, hydrological-cycle, nitrogen-cycle, and broader biospheric dynamics. The scale-matching specifies that the unidirectional-convertibility commitment is held at the scale band where the architectural commitment makes operational sense — biospheric and continental-scale ecozone dynamics — and that finer-scale ecological dynamics are conditioned by the cross-scale coupling specifications the public-check and provisioning bands articulate. The biosphere-and-ecozone-scale specification draws on the nested-scales reference frame Document V.01 articulates: the rights at this scale band are the foundational rights without which the cross-scale architecture cannot hold.

**Feedback condition.** The dual-currency mechanism is itself feedback architecture, in the sense Document XII.02 articulates: the cross-currency translation operates as ongoing signal through which political-economic and ecological dynamics are coupled at constitutive depth. The unidirectional commitment is the feedback architecture's structural commitment — the architecture cannot operate as cultivation condition if the convertibility is bidirectional, because bidirectional convertibility re-establishes the substitution dynamic the architecture is drafted to prevent. Beyond the cross-currency translation, the right's feedback condition specifies ongoing ecological monitoring in the eco-mimetic-monitoring register Document XII.01's Thread G articulates: continuous monitoring of biospheric performance across the scale band, with diverse-epistemic-standing monitoring (Indigenous monitoring institutions; community-based monitoring; technical-scientific monitoring; deliberative-public monitoring) operating at parity. Where the monitoring detects systemic erosion of the unidirectional commitment — incipient bidirectionality, leakage, scope-of-application contraction — the feedback condition triggers the review architecture Document XII.01's Thread G specifies.

**Sunset-or-adaptation condition.** The convertibility-relation specification is subject to scheduled review at the temporal scale appropriate to biospheric-and-ecozone dynamics — not the political-electoral cycle the contemporary American political architecture operates within, but the decades-and-centuries scale the dynamics the right governs operate across. The sunset-or-adaptation condition specifies that the convertibility-relation meta-commitment is reviewed under the adaptive-cycle phase apparatus Document V.01 carries: under conservation-phase conditions the convertibility specification is stabilized with codification work; under release-phase conditions where the architecture's biospheric performance has deteriorated, the convertibility specification can be reviewed under the panarchy framework Document X.01 articulates. The condition does not specify automatic temporal sunset (the commitment is foundational); it specifies the conditions under which scheduled review and amendment are appropriate, coordinated with the reversible-amendment-process right below.

The right's operational specification draws on the comparative-monetary-economics literature where it has begun to engage the architectural commitments dual-currency systems require: Joseph Huber's *Sovereign Money: Beyond Reserve Banking* (2017) on the architectural commitments money-creation governance carries; Mary Mellor's *The Future of Money: From Financial Crisis to Public Resource* (2010) on money-as-public-resource; the heterodox-monetary-economics tradition through Hyman Minsky's *Stabilizing an Unstable Economy* (1986) and Stephanie Kelton's *The Deficit Myth* (2020); and the ecological-economics tradition through Herman Daly's *Beyond Growth: The Economics of Sustainable Development* (1996) and Robert Costanza et al.'s ecological-economics corpus. Document XII.02 carries the engagement with these literatures; this section specifies only the formal bounded-decision-right architecture.

## Reversible Amendment Process as Foundational Decision Right

The reversible amendment process is specified as a foundational decision right with the four-element format. The right operates over the meta-decision of how the constitutional architecture is itself revised, with reversibility specifying that no amendment is engineered-fixture revision but cultivation-condition adjustment under specified feedback architecture and sunset-or-adaptation conditions.

**Scope.** The right operates over the meta-decision of how the constitutional architecture is itself revised — the meta-architectural commitment that no amendment is final, that every amendment carries explicit provision for revision under specified conditions, and that the architecture is cultivated rather than designed-as-final-fixture. The scope is bounded: the right does not specify the content of any particular amendment, nor the political process by which amendments are proposed and adopted (the e-democracy-as-structural-public-check architecture Document XII.03 articulates carries that work). The right specifies only the reversibility meta-commitment as foundational architectural condition.

**Scale.** Biosphere-and-ecozone, where the meta-decision affects the architecture's capacity to register and respond to biospheric-scale disturbances. The reversible-amendment specification holds at the foundational scale because the substantive commitments at finer scales cannot be cultivated under conditions where the meta-architecture is treated as engineered fixture. The cross-scale coupling specifies that the foundational reversibility is what enables the finer-scale architectural commitments to operate as cultivation conditions rather than as fixed-engineered specifications.

**Feedback condition.** Amendment-and-reversal feedback operates through review architecture coordinated with Document XII.03 (E-Democracy and the Structural Public Check). The structural-public-check architecture carries the deliberative substrate through which the population participates in the amendment process at substantive deliberative depth rather than through the thin participation-as-vote register the contemporary American constitutional architecture operates within. The feedback condition specifies that amendment proposals are subject to deliberative engagement at the scale band the amendment operates at, and that proposed amendments include explicit reversibility specifications — what conditions would trigger reversal, what review architecture would conduct the reversal, what cross-scale-coupling considerations the reversal must accommodate — drafted into the amendment text rather than left for later determination. The comparative-constitutional-amendment literature supplies analytical apparatus: Richard Albert's *Constitutional Amendments: Making, Breaking, and Changing Constitutions* (2019) distinguishes the forms of constitutional revision — formal amendment, informal amendment, constitutional dismemberment, constitutional replacement — and positions the amendment process as substantive architectural commitment; Sanford Levinson's *Our Undemocratic Constitution* (2006) articulates the architectural-democratic implications of Article V's near-impossibility threshold and the resulting reliance on judicial reinterpretation as informal amendment.

**Sunset-or-adaptation condition.** Each amendment carries explicit sunset or scheduled-review specification, with the temporal scale of the sunset matched to the scale band the amendment operates at: amendments at biosphere-and-ecozone-scale dynamics carry review cycles measured in decades and centuries; amendments at ecosystem-and-habitat-scale dynamics carry review cycles measured in years and decades; amendments at finer scales carry correspondingly shorter cycles. The condition coordinates with the legislative-cycle architecture Document XII.01's Thread E articulates: review under r-phase conditions, codification under K-phase conditions, managed release under Ω-phase conditions, reorganization under α-phase conditions.

## Judicial Review of Executive Orders as Foundational Decision Right

The judicial review of executive orders is specified as a foundational decision right with the four-element format. The right operates over executive-order outputs as decisions reviewable against the foundational normative commitments Document I.01 specifies, and reads judicial review in the eco-mimetic-feedback register Document XII.01 articulates rather than in the terminal-adjudication register the contemporary American constitutional architecture treats as default.

**Scope.** The right operates over executive-order outputs as decisions reviewable against the Document I.01 foundational normative commitments. The scope specifies that executive decisions — not statutory law (Congress's terrain), not judicial decisions (the court system's terrain), but the unilateral-executive-decision register that has accumulated extra-constitutional reach across the contemporary American constitutional record — are subject to ongoing review against the foundational normative commitments rather than treated as final unless overturned by extraordinary judicial intervention. The scope is bounded: the right specifies the foundational architectural commitment that executive decisions are reviewable as feedback channel rather than as terminal-decision channel.

**Scale.** Biosphere-and-ecozone, where executive decisions have aggregate-impact reach beyond the decision-right scope to which executive orders are formally addressed: climate-policy executive orders, military-and-defense executive orders with environmental reach, trade-policy executive orders with cross-continental supply-chain reach, public-health executive orders with population-scale reach. Executive orders bearing on finer-scale dynamics are conditioned by the rights at the finer scale bands.

**Feedback condition.** Judicial review is read as feedback architecture in the eco-mimetic sense Document XII.01's Thread G articulates: ongoing cross-scale sensing of executive-decision performance against the foundational normative commitments, with diverse-epistemic-standing monitoring at parity, and with multi-disposition outputs (not just uphold-or-strike but also amendment-trigger, adaptation-trigger, succession-trigger, panarchy-cycle-trigger). The specification departs from the contemporary American Article III review architecture, which operates at one institutional scale (the federal court hierarchy), with discrete cases as the trigger and binary uphold/strike outputs. The right specifies that judicial review of executive orders operates as continuous monitoring across scales, with case-triggered review supplemented by standing-monitoring-institution review at the biosphere-and-ecozone scale band — Indigenous monitoring institutions (the Haudenosaunee Grand Council's standing capacity to monitor executive-decision impact on treaty commitments, for instance), community-based monitoring infrastructures, technical-scientific monitoring through the federal scientific apparatus, and deliberative-public monitoring through the Document XII.03 e-democracy architecture. The comparative-constitutional-judicial-review literature supplies architectural apparatus: Mark Tushnet's *Weak Courts, Strong Rights* (2008) articulates weak-form review (the court flagging constitutional concerns without final-veto power, with the legislative branch retaining final-decision authority subject to ongoing review); Stephen Gardbaum's *The New Commonwealth Model of Constitutionalism* (2013) extends the analytical work to the Commonwealth-model jurisdictions; and the transformative-constitutionalism literature through Karl Klare's "Legal Culture and Transformative Constitutionalism" (1998) and Upendra Baxi's *The Future of Human Rights* (2002) articulates the architectural commitments transformative-constitutional review requires.

**Sunset-or-adaptation condition.** Judicial-review outcomes are themselves subject to scheduled review and to the reversible-amendment process above. No judicial-review output is final-engineered-fixture; every output carries explicit provision for review under specified conditions, with the temporal scale matched to the dynamics the executive order operated at. The outputs are not terminal adjudications but cultivation-condition adjustments under the broader architectural cycle.

## Bodily and Reproductive Autonomy as Foundational Decision Rights (sixth-pass thread, preserved)

Document XII.09 carries the sexuality-and-gender-and-reproduction thread at the foundational-rights register: bodily and reproductive autonomy specified as foundational decision rights at biospheric scale. The thread engages the feminist-political-theory tradition, the reproductive-justice movement, the Two-Spirit and Indigenous-gender articulations, and the trans-rights literature, with primary-articulator standing for the Indigenous-gender articulations.

Carole Pateman's *The Sexual Contract* (1988) carries the foundational articulation that the social-contract tradition the dominant constitutional tradition inherits operated under a prior sexual contract subordinating women — that the abstract individual of contract theory was tacitly male, and women's subordination was written into the contract's structure rather than left outside it; the architectural implication is that the social-contract apparatus cannot be treated as adequate substrate for the foundational rights at biospheric scale without engagement with that sexual contract. Catharine A. MacKinnon's *Toward a Feminist Theory of the State* (1989) extends the apparatus into the state's constitutive operation: the state operates not as gender-neutral apparatus over pre-constituted gender categories but as one whose constitutive operations have produced the gender categories under which the architecture operates, so the foundational rights cannot be specified as gender-neutral rights operating over pre-constituted categories. Judith Butler's *Bodies That Matter* (1993) carries the work into the materialization of bodies — the body is not pre-discursive substrate the architecture operates over but is itself materialized through the discursive operations the architecture conducts — and *Notes Toward a Performative Theory of Assembly* (2015) extends it into bodily vulnerability and collective assembly. Adrienne Rich's *Of Woman Born* (1976) distinguishes motherhood as institution from the experience the institution presupposes, and "Compulsory Heterosexuality and Lesbian Existence" (1980) names the enforcement of heterosexuality as architectural commitment rather than natural default.

The reproductive-justice movement, through Loretta J. Ross and Rickie Solinger's *Reproductive Justice: An Introduction* (2017) and the SisterSong corpus, carries the analytical apparatus that reproductive autonomy is not a single negative right (the right not to have children, as the dominant American reproductive-rights discourse framed it through *Roe v. Wade* and its successors) but a three-fold architectural commitment: the right to have children, the right not to have children, and the right to raise children in safety and dignity. The three-fold framing — articulated by Black women organizers in the 1990s and codified through SisterSong in 1997 — engages the structural conditions under which the rights are exercised rather than treating reproductive autonomy as the formal absence of state intervention; it operates as primary articulation, not as supplementary expansion of the dominant framing. The Two-Spirit and Indigenous-gender articulations carry the apparatus the settler-heteropatriarchal framing has concealed. Qwo-Li Driskill (Cherokee), Chris Finley, Brian Joseph Gilley, and Scott Lauria Morgensen, eds., *Sovereign Erotics: A Collection of Two-Spirit Literature* (2011), articulates gender configurations the settler categorization has failed to recognize as anything other than deviation; Kim TallBear (Sisseton Wahpeton Oyate) on Indigenous gender, kinship, and belonging beyond settler heteropatriarchy carries the work into the kinship register, with the Indigenous articulations at primary-articulator standing rather than at comparative-supplementary register. Susan Stryker's *Transgender History* (2008) carries the trans-rights tradition as historical-architectural commitment, and Dean Spade's *Normal Life: Administrative Violence, Critical Trans Politics, and the Limits of Law* (2011) carries the administrative-violence register — the routine administrative apparatus that governs trans lives beneath the level of dramatic legislation, across identity-documentation, healthcare access, housing access, and employment access — positioning the formal-legal-equality framing as inadequate to the architectural commitments the trans-rights movement requires.

**Scope.** The right operates over the person's authority over their own body, gender, and reproduction — the foundational-architectural commitment that bodily, gender, and reproductive decisions reside with the person rather than with the state apparatus, the religious institutional apparatus, the medical-professional apparatus, the employer apparatus, or any other apparatus the contemporary configuration operates through. The scope is bounded: the right does not specify the content of any particular decision, nor the operational architecture by which the medical and social-support infrastructure that conditions the content of the right is provisioned (Scale Band Three specifies the provisioning architecture). The right specifies the foundational meta-commitment that the authority over bodily, gender, and reproductive decisions resides with the person.

**Scale.** Biospheric — at the foundational scale at which the right is held as architectural commitment conditioning the operational specifications at finer scales. The body is the foundational scale of the nested-scales reference frame; the cultivation work proceeds from bodily conditions outward to ecological-scale conditions; and the foundational decision right at the biospheric scale band reads the bodily scale as the foundational scale of the cultivation discipline. The scale-matching coordinates with Scale Band Four (gender and sexuality as organism-scale decision rights), which specifies the organism-scale register; this band specifies the foundational register.

**Feedback condition.** The feedback condition operates through three principal channels: the deliberative-public-feedback channel Document XII.03 articulates, with the architecture's substantive performance for trans persons, Two-Spirit persons, and persons whose reproductive configurations exceed the heteropatriarchal nuclear-family baseline monitored at depth; the diverse-epistemic-standing monitoring Document XII.01's Thread G articulates, with the Indigenous-gender articulations, the reproductive-justice movement, and the trans-rights movement carrying monitoring authority at primary-articulator standing and at parity; and the administrative-architectural monitoring Spade's *Normal Life* identifies as the principal terrain of contemporary autonomy violations — the architecture's performance monitored not only at the formal-legal level but at the administrative level, where the contemporary record carries substantial ongoing violations.

**Sunset-or-adaptation condition.** The right is held as foundational decision right and is not subject to casual sunset; the condition specifies the circumstances under which the right's specification is reviewed (systemic erosion of the right's substantive content under administrative-architectural operations) and adapted (the emergence of new architectural configurations under which the right's operational substrate is reconfigured), at the multi-decade-to-multi-generation scale appropriate to the cultivation work, coordinated with the reversible-amendment-process right above. The right coordinates with Document II.09 (Climate Displacement and Belonging) on the belonging-architecture commitments displacement contexts require, and with Scale Band Three on the provisioning architecture conditioning the right's substantive content.

## Defense, Military, and War Powers, Threaded Through Judicial Review of Executive Orders (sixth-pass thread, preserved, scope-out-with-threading)

The sixth-pass arc treats war and defense as a defensible scope-out from the cultivation framework's center of gravity, threaded through the documents where the question bears on the architecture rather than given standalone home. Scale Band One carries the thread through the judicial-review-of-executive-orders right: executive war-making operates as the paradigm case of an executive decision with biospheric-scale reach that the scale-matched feedback architecture must check.

The constitutional history of war powers articulates a substantive architectural commitment under the original Constitution that the contemporary configuration has substantially eroded. Louis Fisher's *Presidential War Power* (1995, revised) specifies that the original constitutional commitment vested the war-declaration authority in Congress (Article I, Section 8, Clause 11) and the commander-in-chief authority in the President (Article II, Section 2), with the decision to initiate war operating as legislative-deliberative decision. The original architectural commitment is identifiable through the Convention debates (Madison's notes on the August 17, 1787 debate, in which the Convention shifted the verb from "make war" to "declare war" specifically to clarify that the executive retained authority to respond to attack but not to initiate war), through *The Federalist* No. 69 (in which Hamilton specified that the commander-in-chief authority was nothing more than "first general and admiral" authority, distinct from the British Crown's prerogative to declare war), and through the broader Founding-era record. The commitment has been substantially eroded across the twentieth and twenty-first centuries through the Korean War (initiated without congressional declaration in 1950), the Vietnam War (escalated under the Tonkin Gulf Resolution without subsequent declaration), the post-1973 deployments under the War Powers Resolution that have operated under executive interpretation, and the post-September-11 Authorizations for Use of Military Force interpreted as authorizing ongoing operations across multiple decades and theaters. John Hart Ely's *War and Responsibility: Constitutional Lessons of Vietnam and Its Aftermath* (1993) extends the work into the War Powers Resolution of 1973, specifying that the resolution's reporting requirements and the sixty-day-and-thirty-day clocks have been substantially evaded or interpreted to negate the resolution's operational substance. Mary L. Dudziak's *War Time: An Idea, Its History, Its Consequences* (2012) extends the work into the temporal register: "wartime" as analytical category has been made permanent across the post-1945 period, with the implication that executive decisions across the national-security register cannot be reviewed under the assumption that wartime is exceptional condition. *The Federalist* Nos. 69 and 74 (Hamilton, March 1788) on the commander-in-chief clause are read against the Anti-Federalist concerns about standing armies (Centinel, Brutus, and the broader Anti-Federalist corpus, cross-referenced to Document I.03): the original architecture operated under concern about executive war-making as architectural risk, with the commander-in-chief clause drafted under that concern rather than as authorization for executive war-making at the scale the contemporary configuration permits.

Hannah Arendt's *On Violence* (1970) carries the analytical work on the inverse relation between power (the human capacity to act in concert) and violence (the instrumental application of force): power is constituted through concerted action and erodes when displaced by instrumental violence; violence substitutes for power when power has eroded. The implication is that executive war-making operates as substitution of violence for power — the executive's reliance on military operations as architectural method substitutes for the concerted-action register the deliberative apparatus is drafted to operate within. Michael Walzer's *Just and Unjust Wars* (1977), engaged critically, articulates the just-war tradition's content (jus ad bellum, jus in bello, with the later jus post bellum) and supplies analytical resources for reviewing executive war-making against normative criteria; the critical engagement specifies that the just-war tradition operates under the assumption that war is a defensible architectural commitment under specified conditions — an assumption the project's engagement with the pacifist tradition treats as itself subject to architectural review. The pacifist tradition supplies apparatus the just-war tradition cannot accommodate: the historic-peace-church Anabaptist political theology of conscience — engaged through its analytical work rather than through individual scholars whose biographical record disqualifies their personal citation — carries the articulation that war is itself architectural commitment rather than emergency exception, with the conscience-exemption architecture operating as constitutional architecture in the post-Reformation European jurisdictions and the early American republic. Mohandas K. Gandhi's *Hind Swaraj* (1909) carries the *ahimsa* (non-violence) tradition as foundational architectural-political commitment from which the broader political architecture proceeds, with primary-articulator analytical resources for reviewing executive war-making against substantive non-violent-architectural commitments. Roxanne Dunbar-Ortiz's *An Indigenous Peoples' History of the United States* (2014) carries the analytical work on the United States military's role as the principal architectural instrument of Indigenous dispossession from the eighteenth century forward — the Indian Removal Act and its military execution in the 1830s, the post-Civil-War campaigns against the Plains nations, the contemporary military presence on Indigenous lands — and Nick Estes (Lower Brule Sioux), *Our History Is the Future* (2019), extends the work into the contemporary domestic-security register through the Standing Rock resistance. The World Beyond War documentation supplies analytical resource on the contemporary military's biospheric impact: the United States military operates as the single largest institutional consumer of fossil fuels in the contemporary world, with substantial impact across the carbon-cycle, water-cycle, and broader biospheric dynamics Documents II.08 and IX.01 articulate.

The threading specifies that the judicial-review-of-executive-orders right operates as scale-matched feedback architecture for executive war-making decisions, with the feedback condition incorporating the analytical resources the foregoing engagement establishes — the formal-procedural-allocation work (Fisher, Ely, Dudziak), the substantive normative work (Arendt; Walzer engaged critically; the Anabaptist tradition through its analytical work; Gandhi's *ahimsa*), and the Indigenous-architectural-dispossession work (Dunbar-Ortiz, Estes). The right is not a war-powers right per se — the document does not specify war and defense as standalone foundational decision rights, in keeping with the scope-out-with-threading standing — but specifies that executive war-making is the paradigm case of executive decisions the right's scale-matched feedback architecture must check, with the threading operating as analytical commitment rather than as gesture toward a question the document declines to engage.

## E-Democracy as Immediate Public Check, Specified as Bounded Decision Right

**Scope.** The right operates over the public-check capacity to register objection to executive and legislative decisions in near-real-time. The scope is bounded: the right does not specify the substantive content of any particular objection, nor the institutional architecture by which objections trigger reconsideration (Document XII.03 specifies that operational substrate). ## Intergenerational Criterion as Decision Rule as Bounded Decision Right

The bounding work this section conducts is distinguishable from the cultivation work Document XII.04 (Intergenerational Rectification) carries at substantive depth: Document XII.04 articulates what intergenerational rectification substantively looks like as cultivation work; this section specifies the criterion's operation as decision rule at the moment of decision — what makes a proposed decision pass the intergenerational threshold rather than fall below it. Edith Brown Weiss's *In Fairness to Future Generations* (1989) supplies the foundational articulation of intergenerational equity as legal-architectural commitment, with three obligations: conservation of options (preserving the diversity of natural and cultural resources available to future generations); conservation of quality (preserving environmental and cultural quality at no worse than the level inherited); and conservation of access (preserving equitable access to the inheritance). Stephen M. Gardiner's *A Perfect Moral Storm* (2011) extends the work into the convergence of the global storm (asymmetric distribution of climate impact across geographic configurations), the intergenerational storm (asymmetric distribution across generations), and the theoretical storm (inadequacy of the contemporary moral-and-political-theoretical apparatus) — so the criterion must engage the asymmetries rather than operate as generalized future-orientation. Roman Krznaric's *The Good Ancestor* (2020) supplies the contemporary engagement on long-term thinking, with the long-termist tradition engaged critically (its operational record mixed, with substantial debate about how the criterion operates against immediate-justice obligations to currently-existing populations). The Haudenosaunee seventh-generation criterion operates at primary-articulator standing as the most-developed primary articulation of intergenerational criterion as decision rule in continuous practice across centuries; Oren Lyons (Onondaga) and colleagues, eds., *Exiled in the Land of the Free* (1992) and John Mohawk (Seneca), *Utopian Legacies* (2000), supply the primary-articulator engagement. The criterion specifies that decisions are evaluated against their consequences for the seventh generation following the decision — a multi-century forward horizon (approximately 175 to 200 years) operating as decision rule at the moment of deliberation rather than as cultivation framing applied after the fact. The Welsh Future Generations Commissioner architecture (the Well-being of Future Generations (Wales) Act 2015; Sophie Howe, 2016–2023, and Derek Walker, 2023–) supplies the contemporary comparative empirical instance, with seven well-being goals against which public-body decisions are evaluated. The cross-civilizational temporal traditions operate at primary-articulator standing: the Andean *Pachakuti* tradition (Marisol de la Cadena's *Earth Beings*, 2015); the Hopi temporal cosmology (Frank Waters's *Book of the Hopi*, 1963); the Aboriginal Dreamtime tradition (Deborah Bird Rose's *Nourishing Terrains*, 1996); the Buddhist *anicca* (impermanence) tradition; and the Daoist temporal-flow tradition (the *Zhuangzi*, Brook Ziporyn translation, 2020).

## Multi-Lens Checks-and-Balances Feedback Architecture as Bounded Decision Right

The contemporary scholarly literature on the limits of the three-branches architecture is substantial: Bruce Ackerman's *The Decline and Fall of the American Republic* (2010) on how the contemporary architecture has eroded the three-branches checks under the rise of the modern administrative state, the expansion of executive power, and the concentration of political-economic power in extra-governmental institutions; Jack M. # Closing Synthesis — Bounded Decision Rights as the Cultivation-Protocol Decision-Rights Architecture Across Nested Scales
