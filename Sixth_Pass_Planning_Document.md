# Sixth-Pass Planning Document — Closing Coverage Gaps and Preparing the Synthesis

## Opening Note

The sixth pass keeps the load-bearing triad of the fifth pass — biocultural governance as constitutional ontology, eco-mimetic policy-making as operational method, and disturbance-adapted implementation as temporal orientation — and the fourteen-Part Roman-numeraled architecture, and closes the substantive-coverage gaps the fifth-pass audit revealed. The audit found two failure modes. The first was internal: of the thirteen aspects of social life the fifth-pass arc nominally architects through its structural-commitment, material-substrate, and bounded-decision-right documents, none had been drafted to substantive depth — every carrier-document at Phase ζ and Phase η remained in stub form, with the framing layer present but the analytical content gestural. The second was external: the fifth-pass arc inherited the standing omissions of liberal political theory by silently lacking Part-level homes for several aspects of social life that a constitutional architecture must address — health, education, care work, harm response and accountability, climate displacement, labor as cultivation, and land relations as such — and lacked positive homes for sexuality-and-gender, communications-and-press, technology-and-AI governance, arts-and-language, and the war-powers question (whose only fifth-pass appearance was as judicial review of executive orders in D-1).

The sixth pass responds with three categories of work. First, seven new Part-level documents close the gaps where the canon is large enough to warrant standalone treatment: Health as Cultivation (Part VI extension), Pedagogy as Constitutional Cultivation (new placement to be settled), Care, Kinship, and Social Reproduction (Part VI extension), Harm Response, Accountability, and Repair (Part XII extension), Climate Displacement and Belonging (Part II extension), Labor as Cultivation (Part XII extension), and Land Relations and Property as Cultivation (Part VI extension). Second, five cross-document thread additions close the gaps where threading suffices: sexuality-and-gender-and-reproduction as positive aspect (threaded through B, D-3, D-4, J, with comparison in G); communications-and-media-and-press as positive aspect (threaded through F, U, with comparison in G); technology-and-AI as positive aspect (threaded through U, Q); arts-and-language-and-cultural-production (threaded through M, N, B); and defense-and-military-and-war-powers (threaded through D-1, D-2). Third, the twenty-two fifth-pass stubs all get drafted to substantive depth under the expanded architecture, with new threads incorporated where they apply.

The corpus grows from thirty-five active fifth-pass documents to forty-two active sixth-pass documents (forty-three including the preserved historical Document 6, which is documentary and carries no execution plan). A loss-check against the fifth-pass plan during sixth-pass planning corrected a fifth-pass counting error: the fifth-pass plan claimed thirty-seven documents and thirty-four execution plans, but the verifiable figures are thirty-five documents with execution plans plus the historical Document 6. There is no Document 5; the numbered sequence skips it. The total work for the sixth pass is roughly thirty substantial drafting tasks — the seven new documents, the twenty-two stub-to-substantive rewrites (eleven of which carry new thread content), and an expanded G capstone — plus the planning-side work of authoring seven new execution plans and revising eleven existing plans to carry the new threads. # II. THE COVERAGE-GAP AUDIT

The fifth-pass audit conducted before the sixth-pass planning identified two failure modes. The first was internal: a draft-state audit of the corpus found that of the thirty-five active fifth-pass documents, only ten had been drafted to substantive analytical depth — Documents 1, 2, 3, A, M, W, R at full depth, and Documents B, 4, C, I, K, L at moderate depth where filler-density analysis indicated significant gestural-prose content. The remaining twenty-two documents existed as framing-layer stubs (under three thousand words each) or as missing files (D-Synthesis and G). The carrier-documents for every architected aspect of social life were among the stubs. The internal failure mode was that the architecture had been planned thoroughly but executed thinly.

The second failure mode was external: a content audit of the planned aspects of social life found that the fifth-pass arc lacked Part-level homes for several aspects a constitutional architecture must address. Healthcare and public health had no home — D-3 specified material-substrate provisioning rights for shelter, energy, food, water, seed, materials, and repair, but not health, despite health being the most universal material-provisioning question and despite the cultivation framing fitting paradigmatically (bodies are cultivated within ecological, social, and economic conditions, not engineered). Education and pedagogy had no home — pedagogy is *the* primary site of intergenerational cultivation, and engaging neurodivergence at organism scale through Documents J and D-4 while leaving educational architecture unaddressed was internally incoherent. Care work, kinship, and social reproduction had no home — feminist political theory has spent fifty years demonstrating that liberal political theory's omission of care work is structural, and the fifth-pass arc inherited the omission silently while engaging Indigenous kinship-as-constitutive frameworks elsewhere. Harm response, accountability, and repair had no home — the criminal-legal system is the single largest site of contemporary American constitutional contestation, and the abolitionist, restorative-justice, and Indigenous-justice canons are large enough to warrant standalone Part-level treatment. Climate displacement and belonging had no home — the climate disturbance framing logically demands engagement with what may be the largest twenty-first-century population movement, and the polity-formation question of borders and belonging required positive treatment beyond the negative-side disturbance-regime work. Sexuality-and-gender-and-reproduction had only negative-side treatment in the cultural-disturbance diagnostic's embodiment axis; positive treatment of reproductive autonomy, queer kinship, and gender as constitutive of social life had no home. Labor as cultivation had only negative-side treatment in the political-economic-disturbance diagnostic; the positive vision of work — worker self-management, cooperative labor, post-work, dignity-of-work — had no home. Communications, media, and press had only negative-side treatment in the cognitive-and-informational-disturbance diagnostic; the positive vision of press freedom, public-service media, and community media had no home. Technology and AI governance had only partial negative-side treatment; the cultivation-side question of what good technology architecture looks like had no home. Arts, language, and cultural production were implicit in the biocultural framing but had no explicit home. Land relations and property as such were threaded piecemeal through P and N but had no unified treatment, despite property being a foundational liberal constitutional category the project's silence on which is consequential. Defense, military, and war powers had no home — war powers are constitutionally central, allocated in the 1787 document between Congress and the President, and the fifth-pass arc's silence on them was a notable scope-out.

The sixth-pass response moves twelve of these gaps from "silent omission" to "addressed at appropriate weight." Seven gaps receive standalone Part-level treatment as new documents. Five gaps receive cross-document thread treatment following the religious-and-conscience precedent — threads woven across the existing documents at the scales where the aspect operates, with comparison gestures housed in G. Two items remain scope-out: arts-and-language is threaded but does not receive a standalone home because the biocultural framing already carries cultural production at constitutional weight; defense-and-war-powers is threaded through D-1 and D-2 but does not receive a standalone home because its analytical apparatus sits outside the cultivation framework's center of gravity (a future eighth-pass treatment might revisit).

The methodological move underlying the sixth-pass expansion is consistent with the fifth-pass framing. Cultivation does not pick and choose which aspects of social life to cultivate — a constitutional architecture under the cultivation framing must specify cultivation conditions across the substantive terrain of the polity's life. The fifth-pass omissions were not principled scope-outs but inheritance from the design-imposition tradition's blind spots; the sixth-pass corrects the inheritance.

The fourteen-Part architecture remains. Three Parts expand to absorb the sixth-pass new documents; the rest carry forward unchanged in their fifth-pass specifications.

## Part II — Disturbance-Regime Diagnostics and the Implementation Gap (expanded)

Part II grows from eight documents to nine. The Climate Displacement and Belonging document (working designation: Document X) joins the disturbance-pathway documents as a cross-regime diagnostic that operates at the intersection of the physical-environmental regime (Document T) and the political-economic regime (Document V) and that bears centrally on the cultural regime (Document B), the cognitive-and-informational regime (Document U), and the biological regime (Document S). ## Part VI — The Material Substrate (expanded)

Part VI grows from four documents to seven. The Health as Cultivation document, the Care, Kinship, and Social Reproduction document, and the Land Relations and Property as Cultivation document join the four existing material-substrate documents (N built environment, O energy, P food and soil, Q materials). The expansion treats the material substrate as broader than physical infrastructure: it includes the bodily substrate (health), the relational substrate (care and kinship), and the foundational property-relation substrate (land). The Part VI re-conception treats the seven documents as a coherent cluster specifying the cultivation terrain — what the polity provisions and how the provisioning operates as cultivation work — that the D-3 provisioning decision rights instantiate at constitutional weight.

## Part XII — Structural Commitments, Eco-Mimetic Policy-Making, Bounded Decision Rights (expanded)

Part XII grows from nine documents to twelve. Pedagogy as Constitutional Cultivation, Harm Response and Accountability and Repair, and Labor as Cultivation join the nine existing Part XII documents (E currency, F e-democracy, H intergenerational rectification, J neurodivergent polis, R eco-mimetic policy-making, and D-1 through D-4 bounded decision rights). The three additions extend the structural-commitments cluster from a focus on architectural commitments (currency, democracy, intergenerational, cognitive) to include substantive-domain cultivation commitments (pedagogy, harm-response, labor). The Part XII re-conception treats the twelve documents as the substantive-architectural specification of how the polity cultivates the conditions under which biocultural governance proceeds.

The Part-structure placement of Pedagogy and Harm Response remains open at the moment of this planning document. Pedagogy could alternatively sit in Part VI as material-substrate cultivation (treating educational provisioning as material substrate), or in a new Part dedicated to intergenerational-transmission cultivation. Harm Response could alternatively sit as a sub-Part within Part XII or as its own Part. The placement decision settles per the Part-structure-placement consultation deferred from the prior session.

The other eleven Parts (I, III, IV, V, VII, VIII, IX, X, XI, XIII, XIV) carry forward in their fifth-pass specifications. Their internal documents receive cross-reference revisions to point to the new documents and threads.

# XII. SEVENTH-PASS SYNTHESIS AS MILESTONE

The sixth pass produces a complete v6 corpus: forty-two documents drafted to substantive depth, all renamed under the v6 naming convention and admitted to `v6_completed/` through the completeness-check gate, with all fourteen cross-cutting threads woven through, with the expanded architecture realized. The seventh pass is explicitly a synthesis pass and operates as a milestone of the project rather than as routine continuation.

The seventh pass reads the complete v6 corpus end-to-end as a single artifact and performs the following work. First, it identifies cross-cutting tensions, contradictions, and silent gaps that become visible only when the whole corpus is in hand and that no per-document drafting could have surfaced. Some tensions are expected and analytically productive: the cultivation-versus-design framings, the multi-traditional canon's tensions across primary articulators, the disturbance-regime versus structural-commitment framings. Other tensions may emerge as coordination problems requiring targeted revision: a doctrine specified one way in Document E that operates in tension with the same doctrine specified another way in Document D-1, for instance, or a cultivation-framing element articulated thinly in one document that requires strengthening when read against its richer articulation in another. The seventh pass surfaces these systematically.

Second, the seventh pass produces a final-form **Document G v7 capstone** that synthesizes the complete architecture. The current G (under the fifth-pass plan and the sixth-pass expansion of Section VI) carries comparison gestures and integrates the load-bearing triad with the multi-traditional canon and the cross-cutting threads at planning-and-anticipation standing. The v7 G is the actual synthesis: a single document that reads the corpus end-to-end and produces the substantive synthesis-text the project has been building toward. The v7 G inherits the planning standing of the prior G but operates at synthesis standing, with the complete corpus as substrate.

Third, the seventh pass issues targeted revisions to v6 documents where coordination problems become visible. The revisions are surgical: a paragraph here, a sub-section there, a cross-reference revision where the v7 reading reveals that the v6 cross-reference was insufficient. The seventh pass does not produce new full-document drafts (except for G v7); the v6 drafts are the textual substrate the v7 synthesis works with.

Fourth, the seventh pass produces a public-facing derivative artifact: a condensed monograph or essay-form derivative built atop the corpus. The derivative is shorter than the corpus (an estimated two hundred to four hundred pages versus the corpus's thousand-plus pages), is written for an audience that has not read the corpus, and operates as the entry-point or "reader's introduction" to the corpus. The derivative is the form in which the project's substantive analytical work becomes readable beyond the corpus-reader audience.

The seventh pass is the milestone at which the project becomes a fully synthesized constitutional-theoretical work rather than a corpus of related documents. The transition from "corpus" to "work" is what the seventh pass accomplishes. The substantive analytical commitments are produced in the sixth pass; the synthesis-as-work happens in the seventh.

The seventh-pass workload estimate: one to two sessions of corpus-reading at extended-context depth; two to four sessions of G v7 drafting at synthesis standing; an unspecified number of sessions for the targeted revisions to v6 documents (estimated four to eight); five to ten sessions for the public-facing derivative. Total estimated seventh-pass effort is in the range of fifteen to twenty-five sessions.
