## 4. Resolvable tensions

These are productive analytical tensions the corpus holds open by design. They are not flaws; the cultivation discipline's legitimacy partly consists in holding them rather than resolving them. They are listed so a reader knows they are *chosen* rather than *overlooked*.

**T-1 — Renovation versus decolonial settlement.** The corpus carries the strong decolonial critique (Tuck and Yang, Coulthard, Land Back) at primary-articulator standing while its own architecture occupies the renovation position the critique indicts. Held open at synthesis standing (capstone Thread E). Resolvable only by a standpoint commitment the corpus declines to impose; the honest move is to hold it.

**T-2 — Relational ontology versus the rights-bearer form.** The relational ontologies (Indigenous land-as-relation, Ubuntu, Buddhist interdependence) strain the individual-rights-bearer frame the constitutional form supplies. Held open; partially mediated by routing relational and intergenerational standing into the decision-rights feedback conditions rather than only into individual rights.

**T-3 — Universal provisioning rights versus Indigenous land sovereignty.** A universal right-to-land/food/health can read as diluting the particular sovereignty of specific peoples over specific lands. Mediated by locating provisioning authority, on Indigenous land, in Indigenous sovereignty; the tension is held, not dissolved.

**T-4 — Co-equal Indigenous-and-Eastern standing versus the Indigenous center of gravity (see H-1).** This is the tension the increment most directly engaged and partially relieved. It is resolvable by continued deepening (a quantity-of-work question) rather than by a conceptual choice, which is why it is a hole (H-1) *and* a tension: the gap is closable, and until closed the stated parity overstates the realized parity.

**I-2 — In-document insertion labels: RESOLVED by adopting and documenting the bis/ter convention.** The deepening insertions that fall before an existing numbered part are labeled with the "-bis" suffix (e.g. `Part Five-bis`, `Thread C-bis`), applied consistently across all eleven such insertions (`VI.01`, `VI.02`, `VI.03`, `VI.06`, `VI.07`, `II.09`, `IX.01`, `XII.06`, `XII.07`, `XII.08`; `X.01` and `XI.01` took clean trailing ordinals because they insert before an unnumbered closing/integration part). On reflection the bis/ter convention is not a defect to be renumbered away but the *correct* device for this corpus: it is the standard insertion convention of legal and constitutional codification (where §1bis, §2ter are ubiquitous), it is apt for a constitutional-theory project, and — decisively — it avoids the cascade-renumbering of paired Part/Thread documents (notably `VI.01`, with threads A–I and internal "Threads B, C, F, G, H, I" self-references), where renumbering would risk introducing *worse* cross-reference errors than the cosmetic label it removed. The resolution is therefore to adopt the bis/ter convention as the project's documented standard for non-cascade insertions; it is now applied uniformly. (This decision is recorded here and should be reflected in `PROJECT_CONVENTIONS` at the next pass.)

**I-3 — Capstone Thread F's deep-Eastern list: FIXED.** Thread F now carries an eighth-pass note enumerating the full enlarged deep-engagement set (the thirteen documents listed in the Method section above) and pointing to this audit's coverage matrix (H-2), and noting that documents `V.01`, `IV.01`, `VIII.01`, `II.03`, `VI.04`, `VI.05` already carried dedicated Eastern/Islamic engagement. The under-inclusive four-document list is corrected.

**I-4 — Versioning: RESOLVED by explicit decision.** The increment keeps the `_v7` suffix on all new and edited files and describes its content as the "eighth-pass increment." This is a deliberate decision, not an oversight: retaining `_v7` filenames preserves the position-based naming convention and every existing cross-reference, whereas a `_v8` rebadge would churn all thirty-six filenames and every internal citation for no analytical gain. The corpus is therefore "v7 corpus + eighth-pass increment," with the increment's status recorded in this audit, in the capstone's eighth-pass integration notes, and (recommended) in the closeout note. The mixed-version state is thus a documented, deliberate condition rather than an inconsistency.

## 6. Fatal flaws

A fatal flaw would be an internal incoherence or an unmet load-bearing commitment that voids the project's central claim. **The audit finds none of that class.** The central architecture is coherent: the triad, the disturbance diagnostic, the bounded-decision-rights format, the scale-band organization, and the cultivation discipline hang together, and the war-powers increment slots into the existing architecture (meta-disturbance → all five regimes; bounded rights → three bands; sunset condition → the existing four-element format) without strain, which is itself evidence the architecture generalizes. What the audit does record are two **deep vulnerabilities** a serious critic will press, neither of which is fatal but both of which the project must continue to carry honestly rather than claim to have closed:

**V-1 — The appropriation indictment the method cannot fully escape.** The strong decolonial critique holds that no settler-academic synthesis drawing on Indigenous and colonized traditions can be non-extractive, however careful — that primary-articulator standing is still the project choosing whom to carry, and the self-audit is still the extractor grading itself. The project's response (capstone Thread E; Handbook Part Six) is not a refutation but a holding-open: it concedes the renovation position, carries the critique at full strength, and offers honest complicity rather than claimed innocence. Whether this suffices is a standpoint judgment the audit cannot settle. It is the project's deepest vulnerability, and the project's integrity consists precisely in not pretending to have dissolved it. The eighth-pass deepening (H-1) is one concrete response — it materially shifts the engagement from naming traditions to working them — but it does not and cannot convert the project into a non-settler one.

*Eighth-pass increment — the indictment is now given a path, not an answer.* The audit's prior statement that V-1 had no operational response beyond holding-open is superseded: `project/Rights_Holder_Consultation_Consent_and_Co_Authorship_Protocol.md` acknowledges the indictment without defense (the corpus as it stands engages these traditions without the prior consent, authorship, or authority of their rights-holders, which is extraction however careful the drafting) and specifies the free-prior-and-informed-consent process by which the corpus could become a multi-traditional work — graded from attribution through review, edit, co-authorship, and conditional consent to a load-bearing right of withdrawal and refusal, governed by UNDRIP, the CARE principles, OCAP®, the TK/BC Label tooling, and relational accountability, and bound to material commitments (benefit-sharing; support for the land-and-sovereignty demand) so that consultation does not become a settler move to innocence (Tuck and Yang). Crucially, the protocol does not claim to *resolve* V-1: it changes the project's standing — until the process is genuinely undertaken, the corpus is to be described as a settler-authored draft offered for the rights-holders' correction, not as a multi-traditional synthesis — and it relocates the decisive authority (including the authority to make the project worse-off by withdrawing) from the author to the rights-holders, which is the only place a non-self-serving answer to V-1 could come from. V-1 thus moves from "named and held open" to "named, held open, and given a concrete, resourced, refusable path"; it remains a deep vulnerability, not a closed item, because only the rights-holders can close it, and their answer may be no.

**V-2 — Untestedness (H-4 restated as a vulnerability).** The entire architecture is an argument, not a demonstration. Its most important operational claims have never met contact with implementation, and the disturbance-adapted orientation itself implies they would be revised by that contact. A critic is entitled to treat the whole corpus as unfalsified theory. The project's reply — that it offers a cultivation protocol to be grown across generations rather than a finished design to be adopted — reframes the vulnerability but does not remove it.

Neither V-1 nor V-2 is fatal in the sense of internal contradiction; both are the honestly-named outer limits of what a constitutional theory of this kind can claim. The project's chosen posture — hold them open as live disciplines — is, in the audit's judgment, the correct one, and is more defensible than any claimed resolution would be.

## 7. Verdict

The two concrete, well-specified goals of the increment — the war-powers document (G-1) and its threading (G-2) — are **fully realized**, cleanly integrated, and audit-clean (register, cross-references, doubled-word, What-to-Avoid all pass). The open-ended goal — deepen the non-Western traditions (G-3) — is now **substantially realized** for the Eastern, Islamic, and African traditions: the coverage matrix (H-2) shows those columns at dedicated-deep engagement across every document whose subject admits it, with no document left carrying them only at the list level. The honest residual is the *breadth* of "all non-Western traditions" beyond that core (the matrix's "Other" column — Latin American/Andean, Caribbean, Pacific Islander, dalit, Jewish critical traditions — engaged where each document's subject calls for it rather than forced into every document) and the arc's continuing Indigenous center of gravity. The literal "*all* … everywhere" target is not met; the substantive goal is.

The corpus contains **no fatal flaws** of the internal-incoherence class. The four **inconsistencies** (I-1–I-4) are all now **resolved** — the capstone counts and Thread F list are corrected (I-1, I-3), the bis/ter insertion convention is adopted and documented (I-2), and the versioning state is an explicit documented decision (I-4). The cross-reference holes are **closed**: `XII.13` is bidirectionally cross-referenced (H-3), and a previously-undetected Phase-7δ collapsed-range false-negative class (nine artifacts) was found and fixed corpus-wide (H-3a). The non-Western deepening hole (H-1) is largely closed with the residual made precise by the coverage matrix (H-2). The **resolvable tensions** (T-1–T-4) are held open by design. What genuinely remains open is not an error but the outer edge of the project: the breadth residual of H-2's "Other" column (a principled scoping), the untestedness of the operational claims (H-4), and the two **deep vulnerabilities** (V-1 the appropriation indictment, V-2 untestedness) — all honestly named, none fatal, all carried as live disciplines.

In sum: everything the audit uncovered that is *fixable by editing* has been fixed (I-1–I-4, H-3, H-3a, and the H-1 list of genuine list-level gaps). What is left is the irreducible — the principled breadth-versus-depth scoping of the full non-Western canon, the Indigenous center of gravity that follows from the project's own stated commitments, and the untestedness that only practice can resolve. The one remaining cheap housekeeping item is the redundancy-audit re-run over the 36-document corpus (Section 8).
