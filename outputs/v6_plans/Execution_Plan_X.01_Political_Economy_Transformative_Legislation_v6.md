## 2. Load-Bearing Triad

The sixth-pass project rests on a load-bearing triad: **biocultural governance** as constitutional ontology (the polity is a complex adaptive system cultivated across nested spatial and temporal scales as a biocultural formation, with biological substrate and cultural plurality coupled, grounded in the biocultural-diversity and biocultural-rights literatures); **eco-mimetic policy-making** as operational method (ecology, CAS theory, biomimicry, and disturbance-ecology concepts inspire how laws are passed, written, and tested); **disturbance-adapted implementation** as temporal orientation (intergenerational architecture cultivated to absorb a plural disturbance landscape comprising five named regimes — cultural, biological, physical-environmental, cognitive-and-informational, political-economic — and their interactions). The Indigenous and Eastern traditions are the principle's primary articulators; the Western complex-adaptive-systems, ecology, biomimicry, and disturbance-ecology canons are recent and partial recovery. "Grown, not built" is usable as colloquial summary phrase.

For `X.01_Political_Economy_Transformative_Legislation_v6`, the triad bears on how transformative legislation is read under the panarchy-cycle framing as substantive (not metaphorical) analytical category: legislative cycles are read as panarchy cycles in the strict sense; the contemporary moment is diagnosed by where in the adaptive cycle the polity currently sits; the strategic prescription is the cultivation work required to move the polity into a configuration more adequate to the Part IX climate stress case and the Part II disturbance-regimes landscape; the material-substrate documents (N, O, P, Q) and `XII.01_Eco_Mimetic_Policy_Making_v6` (eco-mimetic policy-making) supply the substantive content of transformative legislation under the sixth-pass framing.
