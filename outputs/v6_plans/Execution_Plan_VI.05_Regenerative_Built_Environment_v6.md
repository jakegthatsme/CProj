The thread engages the Pueblo architectural tradition through Rina Swentzell's writings on the Santa Clara Pueblo and Anna Sofaer's Chaco Canyon research; the Haudenosaunee longhouse tradition through John Mohawk, Oren Lyons, and the contemporary Haudenosaunee architectural-revitalization corpus; the Māori marae tradition through Deidre Brown's *Maori Architecture* (2009); the Pacific Islander stilt-house and *fale* traditions through Albert Refiti's *Mavae and Tofiga* (2014); and the Hawaiian *hale* tradition through Davianna McGregor's *Nā Kuaʻāina* (2007). ### Thread E — East Asian and Islamic traditional architectural traditions as primary articulators

The thread engages the Chinese tradition through Ji Cheng's *Yuanye* (c. 1631), the feng shui literature through Stephen Field and Ole Bruun's *An Introduction to Feng Shui* (2008), and Ronald Knapp's *China's Old Dwellings* (2000); the Japanese tradition through the tea-house (*chashitsu*) tradition via Okakura Kakuzō's *The Book of Tea* (1906) and the Japanese carpentry tradition via Azby Brown's *The Genius of Japanese Carpentry* (2013); and the Islamic architectural tradition through Hassan Fathy's *Architecture for the Poor* (1973), the Aga Khan Trust for Culture publications, the medina-and-mahalla form through Janet Abu-Lughod's *Cairo* (1971), the courtyard typology through Brian Edwards's *Courtyard Housing* (2006), and the bazaar tradition through Mohammad Gharipour's *The Bazaar in the Islamic City* (2012). These traditions stand at primary-articulator standing.

### Thread F — Arts, language, and cultural production and the built environment (sixth-pass addition)

The thread carries the sixth-pass arts-and-language-and-cultural-production thread, specifying the built environment as cultivation terrain for cultural production — the spaces in which arts, language, and cultural practice are made, transmitted, and sustained. It engages Raymond Williams's *Culture and Society* (1958) and *The Country and the City* (1973) on the relation of cultural production to its material and spatial conditions; Stuart Hall's cultural-studies corpus on cultural production as a site of contestation; the public-arts and community-arts literature through Suzanne Lacy's *Mapping the Terrain: New Genre Public Art* (1995) on art made in and for shared space; bell hooks's *Art on My Mind* (1995) on the place of art in everyday and community life; and the language-and-cultural-sovereignty literature on the architecture of language revitalization — the Kōhanga Reo language-nest and the marae as built spaces of cultural and linguistic transmission, engaged at primary-articulator standing and coordinated with the marae treatment of Thread D. The thread specifies that a built environment cultivated as living structure must cultivate the spaces of cultural production — studios, gathering places, libraries, language nests, performance and ceremonial space — as a constitutional rather than an amenity question, coordinating with `III.01` and `II.02`, the other arts/culture carriers, and housing comparison gestures in `XIV.01`.
