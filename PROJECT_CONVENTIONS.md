## 2. Normative Commitments

These are load-bearing and not re-argued during drafting. They carry forward from the fifth pass and remain unchanged at the sixth pass:

- **Biocultural governance** as constitutional ontology — the polity is a complex adaptive system cultivated across nested spatial and temporal scales as a biocultural formation; biological substrate and cultural plurality coupled, neither reducible to the other.
- **Eco-mimetic policy-making** as operational method — ecology, complex-adaptive-systems theory, biomimicry, and disturbance-ecology concepts inspire the architecture by which laws are passed, written, and tested.
- **Disturbance-adapted implementation** as temporal orientation — cultivated across intergenerational scales to absorb, metabolize, and recover from a plural disturbance landscape (five named regimes: cultural, biological, physical-environmental, cognitive-and-informational, political-economic).
- **Cultivation, not design-imposition.** The constitutional architecture is grown under cultivation conditions, not engineered or specified in advance.
- **Indigenous and Eastern traditions as primary articulators**, not comparative supplement. The Western complex-adaptive-systems, ecology, biomimicry, and disturbance-ecology canon is recent and partial recovery of what the primary articulators already specify.
- **Multi-traditional canon at co-equal weight.** Indigenous, Eastern, African, Islamic, Latin American, Caribbean, Pacific Islander, Jewish, dalit, Black-radical, disability-justice, and neurodivergent traditions stand in their own analytical registers.
- **Intergenerational temporal frame** as foundational; Haudenosaunee seventh-generation criterion as primary articulation.
- **Sixth-pass principle: cultivation does not pick and choose.** A constitutional architecture under the cultivation framing must specify cultivation conditions across the substantive terrain of the polity's life. The fifth-pass omissions were not principled scope-outs but inheritance from the design-imposition tradition's blind spots; the sixth pass corrects the inheritance.

Positions the project explicitly rejects:

- Design-imposition framings of constitutional theory.
- Synthesis-into-architecture across the multi-traditional canon (treating the canon as raw material to be synthesized into one architectural register).
- Comparative-supplement treatment of non-Western traditions.

## 5. Stylistic Conventions

- **Register:** Scholarly. Multi-thousand-word analytical prose with integrated citations. The Phase α v5 exemplars (Documents 1, 2, 3) and the substantively-drafted v5 exemplars (Documents A, M, W, R) are the calibration anchors.
- **Citation format:** Integrated into the sentence with author, work, date on first mention. For Indigenous scholars, tribal affiliation in parentheses where the scholar identifies. References list at end of each document.
- **Length discipline:** `completeness-not-length`. No word counts, page counts, or length-target framing in drafts. Analytical commitments met, not word-floors hit.
- **No bullets in analytical sections.** Lists permitted only for inventories (references, primary sources). Argument carried in prose.
- **No business-prose apparatus.** No executive summaries, no key-takeaways boxes, no TL;DR.
- **The "what survives / what does not" analytical move** adapted to each document's task.
- **"Primary articulators / Western recovery" analytical move** as the project's signature engagement with the multi-traditional canon.
- **First-mention conventions are load-bearing:** full name, work title, year. Indigenous scholars with tribal affiliation. Other identity markers where analytically relevant.
- **Cultivation framing replaces design-imposition framing** in all drafting registers. Watch for slippage into "engineered," "specified in advance," "designed."

## 6. Filler-Discipline Audit (Sixth-Pass Addition)

The fifth-pass execution surfaced a load-bearing failure mode: filler-density inflation of project-specific terms used as gestural padding rather than as load-bearing terminology. The pattern was most visible with "substantive" — documents at framing-layer-stub state reached densities of seventeen to forty-one hits per thousand words for "substantive" alone, against substantively-drafted documents that ran one to eight hits per thousand words. The pattern is not unique to "substantive"; any project-specific term can become filler when overused as gestural padding.

The sixth-pass discipline:

- Every drafted document and every drafted execution plan runs the filler-density audit at completion. The audit checks at minimum the following candidate terms: `substantive`, `analytical`, `framing`, `cultivation`, `tradition`, `canon`, `literature`, `primary`, `articulator`, `engagement`, `thread`, `biocultural`. Other candidates can be added as the drafting reveals overuse patterns.
- The audit reports each term's count and density (per one thousand words). Densities above ten per one thousand words receive close inspection: if the term is load-bearing semantic content (e.g., "thread" in a document centrally about adding threads), the density is acceptable; if the term is gestural padding, the document is revised to substitute or remove.
- For "substantive" specifically: target under five per one thousand words on any drafted document. Above eight is a strong signal of filler. Above fifteen is failure.
- The audit also runs a doubled-word scan (`\b(\w+)\s+\1\b`) to catch artifacts of inline cleanup that left duplicated adjacent words.
- The audit runs automatically as part of the per-document drafting protocol (Section 7 below).

## 7. Completeness-Check Gate and Loss-Check Protocol (Sixth-Pass Addition)

The sixth pass is a completeness-gated migration. Every one of the forty-two active documents — including the seven substantively-drafted fifth-pass documents (1, 2, 3, A, M, W, R) — passes a completeness check before it is renamed under the v6 convention (Section 8) and admitted to the `v6_completed/` folder. The completeness check is the gate: a document that fails the check does not enter `v6_completed/` until it is remediated and re-checked. The historical Document 6 is exempt (no execution plan, no required threads); it is renamed and preserved but does not pass through the gate.

### Completeness-Check Criteria

A document passes the completeness check when all of the following hold:

1. Every Required Thread named in the document's execution plan is executed in the draft.
2. Every primary articulator named in the plan is engaged at primary-articulator standing, with tribal or identity affiliation where applicable and full first-mention conventions (author, work, year).
3. Every cross-reference in the plan is present, concrete, and resolvable to a real document in the arc.
4. The filler-density audit (Section 6) passes: no candidate term above its failure threshold; "substantive" under five per one thousand words.
5. The loss-check (below) passes: nothing load-bearing from the execution plan, the v6 plan, or the v5 plan has been silently dropped.
6. The doubled-word scan returns zero.
7. The scholarly register matches the Phase α v5 exemplars (Documents 1, 2, 3) on calibration — integrated citation, no bullets in analytical sections, multi-traditional primary-articulator standing.
8. The draft conforms to the plan's What-to-Avoid section.

A document that passes is renamed under the Section 8 convention and written to `v6_completed/`. A document that fails is remediated against the failing criterion and re-checked. The completeness-check report accompanies each document: a short note recording each of the eight criteria as pass or fail, and for any fail, what was remediated.

### Loss-Check Protocol (Criterion 5, detailed)

- For new sixth-pass execution plans: cross-check against the v5 plan template (per the `execution-plan-writing` skill) to ensure all ten sections are present and that no v5-spec material is dropped. Sections: Document Identification; Load-Bearing Triad; Skills to Load; Substantive Task; Required Threads; Source Requirements; Structural Requirements; Cross-References; Output Requirements; What to Avoid.
- For revised sixth-pass execution plans (the eleven plan revisions): cross-check against the existing v5 plan to ensure that all v5 content is preserved except for the explicitly-revised thread additions; verify that new thread content integrates with existing thread content without contradiction.
- For drafted v6 documents: cross-check against the relevant execution plan to ensure every Required Thread, every primary articulator, every cross-reference, and every What-to-Avoid item is honored. Cross-check against `Fifth_Pass_Planning_Document.md` Section I (Roman-numeraled architecture) and Section III (cross-cutting threads) to ensure the v5 framings the document inherits are preserved. For the seven substantively-drafted v5 documents passing through the gate, cross-check the existing draft against its v6 execution plan (including any new thread additions) and against the v5 framings to confirm completeness at v6 standing.
- The loss-check is also run against the cross-pass count and arc integrity: confirm the document's Part placement and ordinal position match the Section VII naming map, and that no document has been silently added or dropped from the forty-two-document arc.
- For any deliverable where the loss-check reveals a dropped item, the deliverable is revised to restore the item before submission.

This protocol caught the fifth-pass document-count miscount (claimed thirty-seven, verifiable thirty-five with plans plus the historical Document 6) during sixth-pass planning. The protocol runs on every deliverable precisely to catch this class of silent loss.

### Naming (Sixth-Pass Convention)

The sixth pass drops fifth-pass document numbers and letters in favor of a position-based naming convention. Document numbers and letters are not preserved.

- **Document filenames:** `<RomanNumeral>.<NN>_<Title>_v6.md`, where `<RomanNumeral>` is the Part (I through XIV), `<NN>` is the two-digit ordinal position within the Part in reading order (01, 02, …), and `<Title>` is the short title with words joined by underscores. Examples: `I.01_Declaration_as_Foundation_v6.md`; `VI.01_Land_Relations_and_Property_v6.md`; `XII.01_Eco_Mimetic_Policy_Making_v6.md`. The full forty-two-document naming map is in `Sixth_Pass_Planning_Document.md` Section VII.
- **Execution plan filenames:** `Execution_Plan_<RomanNumeral>.<NN>_<Title>_v6.md`, matching the document's v6 name. Example: `Execution_Plan_VI.06_Health_as_Cultivation_v6.md`.
- **Historical Document 6:** `Historical_Phase_One_Synthesis.md` — no Part ordinal, since it sits outside the active arc and the completeness-check gate.
- **Renaming on gate passage:** a document is renamed under this convention at the moment it passes the completeness-check gate (Section 7) and is written to `v6_completed/`. Before that, drafts may carry working names. The fifth-pass designation is recorded in the document's header and in the Section VII naming map for traceability only; it is not part of the filename.
- **No `_v6_edits` files:** because all forty-two active documents — including the seven substantively-drafted v5 documents — pass through the completeness-check gate and are written fresh into `v6_completed/` under the new name, there is no separate edits-file mechanism. The substantively-drafted v5 documents are migrated by running them through the completeness check (which may surface remediation), applying any v6 thread additions and cross-reference revisions, and writing the result under the new v6 name.
- **File format:** Markdown, no front-matter (the fifth-pass designation may appear in a header line for traceability).
- **Delivery method:** `present_files` followed by brief two-to-four-sentence contextualizing commentary plus the completeness-check report. The document is the artifact; long post-presentation summaries are avoided.

## 9. Workflow Conventions

- **Edit-not-rebuild as default.** Targeted edits over rewrites for any file or artifact that already exists.
- **Move-don't-regenerate** for relocations work and preserved-with-edits documents. Substantive content is preserved; targeted edits add, extend, or re-frame at specified insertion points.
- **Direct-to-output drafting** for single-file deliverables. Write to the v6 output path from the start; verify only at meaningful checkpoints.
- **Segment-to-disk** (long-research-splitting) only for very long documents at session-timeout risk or where the user has explicitly asked for segments. Segment files go to `/home/claude/` and are concatenated to the final output path on completion.
- **Cross-document citation discipline.** Documents cite earlier-phase v6 documents by reference; they do not duplicate the analytical work earlier documents have done.
- **Pre-drafting consultation:** the plan is the consultation. For documents whose execution plan is fully authored, skip the four-check consultation — the plan supplies the equivalent specification. For new sixth-pass plans (the seven new documents' plans authored under v6), the consultation runs because the plan itself is the deliverable.
- **Ambiguity escalation.** When the plan, source, cross-referenced document, or relocations file disagree, stop and ask. Do not guess. Format specified in `CLAUDE.md`.
- **Per-document checkpoint** for consequential and re-specified documents. Batched checkpoint (up to four) only for clean cross-reference-revision propagation. Mandatory single-checkpoint for the seven new sixth-pass documents (Climate Displacement, Land, Health, Care, Pedagogy, Harm Response, Labor), for the capstone (XIV.01), the decision-rights synthesis (XIII.01), the constitutional-ontology document (III.01), and any document where ambiguity arose. All forty-two active documents additionally require the completeness-check report (Section 7) at the gate.
- **Phase-transition note** at the boundary between phases: brief re-read note confirming the Phase α v5 exemplars have been consulted for register calibration.
- **Filler-discipline audit** (Section 6) runs at completion of every drafted document and every drafted execution plan.
- **Loss-check protocol** (Section 7) runs at completion of every v6 deliverable.
- **Model:** Opus 4.7 throughout drafting work; the calibration of the scholarly register against the multi-traditional canon at primary-source depth was done on Opus and drifts on shallower models.

## 10. Self-Contained Execution-Plan Requirement (Sixth-Pass Addition)

Every sixth-pass execution plan must be completely self-contained. The plan operates as a standalone drafting specification readable without external context other than the named source files and cross-referenced execution plans for upstream documents. The plan must:

- **Fire the appropriate skills** in its opening section. The plan names every skill required to draft the document under v6 conventions, in load order: at minimum `scholarship-mode`, `completeness-not-length`, `direct-to-output-drafting`, `usage-efficiency`. Additional skills where relevant: `pre-drafting-consultation` (for new documents under unsettled framing), `long-research-splitting` (for documents at session-timeout risk).
- **Specify the load-bearing triad** under which the document operates, naming biocultural governance, eco-mimetic policy-making, and disturbance-adapted implementation.
- **Name the substantive task** with sufficient specificity that a fresh Claude Code session can draft the document from the plan alone.
- **Specify every Required Thread** including the new sixth-pass threads where they apply to the document.
- **Name every primary articulator** at primary-articulator standing, with tribal affiliation for Indigenous scholars.
- **Specify source requirements** with explicit paths for v4 substrate, v5 substrate, and any holding files.
- **Specify structural requirements** including the no-bullets-in-analytical-sections discipline, the no-business-prose-apparatus discipline, and the citation conventions.
- **Specify cross-references** to upstream and downstream documents by document designation and analytical role.
- **Specify output requirements** including the v6 output path and the filename convention.
- **Specify what to avoid** including the filler-discipline failure modes specific to the document's analytical terrain.
- **Specify the filler-discipline audit terms** the document should run at completion, including any document-specific candidate terms beyond the standard twelve.
- **Specify the loss-check protocol** the document should run, naming the upstream documents and planning sections to cross-check against.

Plans authored under this requirement do not depend on Claude reading the planning document at draft time. The plan is the consultation; the document is drafted from the plan.

## 11. Seventh-Pass Synthesis Milestone

The seventh pass is the project milestone at which the v6 corpus becomes a unified theoretical work rather than a collection of related documents. The seventh-pass work is specified in `Sixth_Pass_Planning_Document.md` Section XII. The work includes corpus-wide end-to-end reading, tension-and-contradiction identification, final-form Document G v7 capstone authoring, targeted revisions to v6 documents where coordination problems emerge, and a public-facing condensed monograph or essay-form derivative.

The seventh-pass is treated as a distinct project phase. It does not commence until the sixth pass is substantively complete (all forty-two active documents drafted at substantive depth, passed through the completeness-check gate, and admitted to `v6_completed/`, all fourteen threads woven through, the v6 corpus available as a single end-to-end readable artifact). Conventions for the seventh-pass venue, model, and workflow will be specified in a seventh-pass planning document authored when the sixth pass nears completion.
